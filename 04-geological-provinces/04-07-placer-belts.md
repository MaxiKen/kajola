# 4.7 — Coastal & River Placer Belts

> **Type:** Secondary (placer) concentration deposits · **Settings:** coastal beach sands + river/terrace placers
> **Quick summary:** Where weathering and water **concentrate dense minerals** into workable deposits — coastal **heavy-mineral (black) sands** and **river/alluvial placers**.

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deposit type | **Placer** (secondary concentration) |
| Settings | Coastal **beach sands**; river/terrace **alluvial** placers |
| Coastal minerals | Ilmenite, rutile, **zircon, monazite**, magnetite ("black sands") |
| River minerals | **Tin & columbite** (Jos); **gold** (schist belts) |
| Mechanism | Dense minerals sorted & concentrated where water slows |
| Economic role | Titanium, zircon, rare-earth (monazite); artisanal tin & gold |

## Overview
Where weathering and water **concentrate dense minerals** into workable deposits. Two settings: **coastal beach sands** and **river/terrace placers**. Placers are "secondary" deposits — minerals weathered out of hard rock and re-concentrated by water.

## Coastal heavy-mineral (beach) sands
Concentrate **ilmenite, rutile, zircon, monazite, and magnetite** (the "black sands").
- **States:** **Lagos, Ondo, Delta, Rivers, Akwa Ibom, Cross River.**
- Resources: **titanium** (ilmenite/rutile), **zircon**, and **rare-earth** (monazite) (see [Titanium minerals](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/titanium-minerals.md), [Zircon & Monazite](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/zircon-monazite.md)).

## River / alluvial placers
- **Tin & columbite** in the **Jos Plateau** rivers — **Plateau, Bauchi** (see [Cassiterite](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/cassiterite.md)).
- **Gold** in rivers draining the schist belts — **Zamfara, Osun, Oyo, Kwara, Niger, Kebbi, Edo** (see [Gold](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/gold.md)).

## Geology & formation mechanism
Placers form because **dense, durable minerals** (high specific gravity, resistant to weathering) are **sorted and concentrated by water**. As river flow or waves slow (river bends, beaches, terrace gravels), heavy minerals settle out while light material is washed away — a natural gravity-concentration process.

## Key minerals & resources
- **Titanium** (ilmenite, rutile), **zircon**, **monazite (REE)** — coastal black sands.
- **Cassiterite (tin)**, **columbite** — Jos Plateau rivers.
- **Gold** — rivers draining the schist belts.
- **Magnetite** — a common by-product of black sands.

## Notable localities
- **Coastal black sands** — Lagos, Ondo, Delta, Rivers, Akwa Ibom, Cross River.
- **Jos Plateau** rivers — tin/columbite placers.
- **Schist-belt** rivers — gold placers.

## Geological significance
Placers are among the **easiest minerals to mine** (loose sand/gravel, no hard-rock crushing). Nigeria's coastal **black sands** are a significant, underexploited source of **titanium, zircon, and rare earths**, while river **gold** and **tin** placers support large artisanal industries.

## Exploration notes
- **Pan streams** and sample **black sands**; follow heavy minerals upstream.
- Placers form where water **slows** (river bends, beaches, terrace gravels) (see [Placers & alluvium](../02-rocks-of-nigeria/sedimentary/placers-alluvium.md)).

## Field tips
- **Pan** stream or beach sand; dense heavy minerals concentrate in the pan bottom.
- Follow heavy minerals (gold, cassiterite) **upstream** to the lode source.
- Separate **ilmenite** (black streak) from **rutile** (pale streak) and **magnetite** (magnetic).

## Facts
- Placers form where water **slows**, concentrating dense minerals.
- Nigeria's coastal **black sands** hold **titanium, zircon, and monazite (REE)**.
- **Tin/columbite** placers concentrate in **Jos Plateau** rivers.
- **Gold** placers drain the **schist belts** (Zamfara, Osun, etc.).
