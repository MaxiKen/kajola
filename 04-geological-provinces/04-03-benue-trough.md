> **Type:** Cretaceous rift/sag basin · **Age:** Cretaceous

## Overview
A vast **NE–SW Cretaceous rift/sag basin** (~800 km) that splits the Basement Complex into a northwestern and southeastern half. It holds an exceptional **diversity** of resources — coal, lead–zinc, salt, limestone, iron, barite, and more.

## Location & extent
**Benue, Kogi, Enugu, Ebonyi, Nasarawa, Plateau (south), Taraba, Cross River, Anambra.** The trough runs ~800 km NE–SW, dividing the Basement into NW and SE halves.

## Geology
- **Cretaceous sediments**: Bima Sandstone (continental), then marine/paralic **Yolde, Abakaliki, and Enugu** formations (sandstone, shale, limestone, coal).
- Folded and faulted along its margins (see [Sedimentary basins](../01-general-geology/01-04-sedimentary-basins.md)).
- The sequence records the basin's transition from continental rifting to marine incursion.

## Structure & tectonic setting
A **rift/sag basin** — a failed arm of a Cretaceous rift system, later folded and faulted along its margins. This structural setting localizes mineralizing fluids (Pb–Zn, barite) and creates traps for sediment-hosted deposits.

## Key minerals & resources
- **Coal** — Enugu, Kogi (Ogboyaga), Nasarawa (Lafia/Obi) (see [Coal](../03-mineral-resources/energy/coal.md)).
- **Lead–zinc** — Abakaliki, Ishiagu (Enugu), Zurak (Plateau) (see [Galena & Sphalerite](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/galena-sphalerite.md)).
- **Salt** — Awe (Nasarawa); **Limestone** — Nkalagu, Gboko, Igalamela (see [Limestone](../03-mineral-resources/industrial/limestone.md)).
- **Iron ore** — Itakpe, Agbaja, Ajabanoko (Kogi) (see [Iron ore](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/iron-ore.md)).
- **Barite, gypsum, clay, phosphate, uranium**, and bitumen shows (see [Barite](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/barite.md)).

## Notable localities
- **Enugu** — "Coal City," Nigeria's main coal district.
- **Abakaliki / Ishiagu** (Enugu/Ebonyi) — lead–zinc.
- **Itakpe** (Kogi) — iron ore (Ajaokuta steel complex).
- **Awe** (Nasarawa) — rock salt.

## Geological significance
The Benue Trough hosts Nigeria's most **diverse** suite of sedimentary mineral resources, spanning energy (coal), metals (Pb–Zn, iron), industrial (limestone, barite, salt, gypsum, clay, phosphate), and even uranium. Its rift/sag history created the source–reservoir settings for these deposits.

## Exploration notes
- Sediment-hosted deposits — use **geology mapping, geochemistry, and drilling**.
- Follow **gossans and quartz/calcite veins** for Pb–Zn; assess **coal seams** and **limestone** beds; evaporites for salt/gypsum.

## Field tips
- For **Pb–Zn**, follow rusty **gossans** and quartz/calcite veins in the limestones.
- For **coal**, map the sandstone/shale "coal measures" and assess seams.
- For **evaporites** (salt/gypsum), target the basin's evaporitic intervals.

## Facts
- The Benue Trough is a ~800 km **Cretaceous rift/sag basin**.
- It **splits the Basement** into NW and SE halves.
- It hosts an **exceptional diversity** of resources (coal, Pb–Zn, iron, salt, limestone, barite, and more).
- **Enugu** ("Coal City") and **Abakaliki** (Pb–Zn) are its most famous districts.
