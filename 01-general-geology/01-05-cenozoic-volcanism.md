# 1.9 — Cenozoic Volcanism (Jos & Biu Plateaus)

Much younger than the granites, Nigeria also has **Tertiary-to-Recent volcanic
rocks** — mostly **basalt** — erupted long after the Jurassic granites.

## The Jos "Younger Basalts"

Patches of **basalt lava flows** cap parts of the **Jos Plateau** and nearby areas.
They are geologically young (Pliocene to possibly Pleistocene), erupted long after
the granites they rest on. You'll see them as dark, fine-grained rock overlying the
pale granite.

- **States:** **Plateau, Bauchi, Kaduna** (edges).
- **Use:** construction aggregate, road metal, building stone.

## The Biu Plateau (Borno)

A large basalt-covered plateau (~4,000 km²) in **Borno State** (near Biu and
Askira-Uba), formed by **Tertiary (Miocene–Pliocene)** lava flows. It is Nigeria's
outlying expression of the great **Cameroon Volcanic Line** that runs down to
Mount Cameroon.

- **States:** **Borno, Yobe** (margins).
- **Features:** young basalt flows, cinder cones, and columnar-jointed basalt.
- **Note:** the volcanic rocks can host minor mineralization and influence local
  groundwater.

## The Cameroon Volcanic Line (context)

The Biu Plateau is the northern tip of a **~1,600 km chain of volcanoes** running
from the Gulf of Guinea (Mount Cameroon) inland through Biu. It is an anomalous,
intra-plate volcanic line — a reminder that West Africa's Cenozoic geology is not
entirely quiet.

## Recognizing basalt in the field

| Feature | What to look for |
|---------|------------------|
| **Colour** | Dark grey to black (mafic, rich in Fe-Mg minerals) |
| **Texture** | Fine-grained (aphanitic); crystals too small to see |
| **Vesicles** | Often bubbly (vesicular) where gas escaped |
| **Magnetism** | Commonly **magnetic** (magnetite) — test with a hand magnet |
| **Joints** | **Columnar jointing** (hexagonal columns) in thick flows |
| **Contact** | Sharp contrast with the pale granite it overlies |

> **Field note.** Basalt is **dark, fine-grained, sometimes vesicular** (bubbly),
> and often **magnetic** — an easy contrast to the pale, coarse granite it may
> overlie. Full ID in **Part II** (basalt) and **§0.4**.

## Other minor occurrences

Scattered **Tertiary intrusives and volcanics** occur elsewhere, but the Jos and
Biu basalts are the headline features.

## Economic relevance

- **Construction aggregate & road metal** — the main use of the basalts.
- **Groundwater** — basaltic aquifers and springs.
- **Minor mineralization** — some volcanic/associated rocks carry trace metals.
- **Dimension/landscape stone** — columnar basalt is decorative.

## Facts & tips

- **Fact:** Nigeria's volcanism is **Cenozoic** (tens of millions of years old) —
young by geological standards, though there are no active volcanoes today.
- **Fact:** the **Biu Plateau** links Nigeria to the Cameroon Volcanic Line, an
  unusual intra-plate volcanic chain.
- **Tip:** basalt's **magnetism** and dark colour make it a quick field ID — and a
  useful marker when mapping over granite.
- **Tip:** columnar jointing is a photogenic, diagnostic feature of thick basalt
  flows (look for it on the Biu Plateau).
