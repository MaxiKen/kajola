# 1.11 — Regolith, Weathering & Laterite

Nigeria is **hot and wet**, so rocks **weather deeply and fast**. Understanding this
weathered layer — the **regolith** — is essential, because it both **hides bedrock**
and **concentrates ores**.

## Why it matters

- Much of the Basement Complex is buried under **tens of metres** of weathered rock
  and soil. Outcrop is patchy — which is why **geochemistry and geophysics** carry
  much of the exploration load here.
- Weathering **releases and concentrates** dense/insoluble minerals into **placers**
  and **residual deposits** — a big deal for **tin, gold, and heavy minerals**.

## The laterite profile

A typical tropical weathering profile, from the surface down:

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────┐
  │  Topsoil / lateritic soil    │  organic-rich, roots
  │  Ferricrete (iron duricrust) │  hard iron-rich cap
  │  Mottled / pallid zone       │  pale kaolinitic clay
  │  Saprolite (rotten rock)     │  retains rock structure
  │  Fresh bedrock               │  unweathered parent rock
  └──────────────────────────────┘
```

### What happens in each zone

| Zone | Process | What it does to minerals |
|------|---------|--------------------------|
| **Topsoil** | Organic + leaching | Roots mix material; metals can be stripped |
| **Ferricrete** | Iron accumulation | Hard cap; can carry **iron, gold, bauxite** |
| **Mottled/pallid** | Intense leaching (kaolinization) | Pale clay; **kaolin** forms here |
| **Saprolite** | Rotting in place | Retains texture; **residual ores** concentrate |
| **Fresh rock** | Unweathered parent | The bedrock source / lode |

## Geochemical dispersion

Weathering spreads a deposit's chemical signature through the regolith — this is what
makes **geochemistry** work:

- **Primary dispersion** — in the ore itself.
- **Secondary (clastic) dispersion** — fragments of ore moved mechanically into soil.
- **Hydromorphic dispersion** — metals dissolved and re-precipitated as a "halo."

Dense minerals (gold, cassiterite, magnetite) disperse **mechanically**; others
(copper, zinc) disperse **chemically**. Knowing which controls how you sample.

## Sampling the regolith

- **Soil grids** — regular spacing; the **−80 mesh** fraction is standard.
- **Profile pits / auger** — sample the **right horizon** (often the
  saprolite/laterite interface).
- **Termite mounds** — a cheap "deep" sample (termites bring up subsoil).
- **Panning** — for heavy minerals in soils and streams.

## Economic consequences

- **Placer tin & columbite** — dense cassiterite/columbite released from pegmatites
  concentrate in soils and streams (Jos Plateau).
- **Residual/lateritic gold** — gold can be enriched near the base of the profile.
- **Kaolin & clay** — intense feldspar weathering makes world-class **kaolin**.
- **Lateritic iron & bauxite potential** — iron (and locally aluminium) enrichment.
- **Heavy-mineral beach sands** — along the coast (zircon, rutile, ilmenite, monazite).

## Laterite types (for field recognition)

- **Ferricrete** — hard, iron-rich duricrust (red/brown, nodular).
- **Bauxitic laterite** — aluminium-rich (pale, pisolitic) — the bauxite precursor.
- **Kaolinitic (pallid) clay** — pale, soft, leached.

## Exploration implications

- Sample the **right horizon** — placers and residual ores sit at particular depths.
- Deep weathering can put ore minerals far from their bedrock source — **panning and
  soil geochemistry** still work, but be aware of **dispersion**.
- Expect to use **augering, pitting, and drilling** to reach fresh rock and confirm.

## Tips & facts

- **Tip:** sample a **consistent horizon** across a survey — mixing horizons makes
  geochemical noise.
- **Tip:** in **gold** ground, coarse gold causes a "nugget effect" — take larger
  samples and field duplicates.
- **Fact:** Nigeria's deep laterite is both a **challenge** (hides ore) and an
  **opportunity** (concentrates it).
- **Fact:** the world's richest **kaolin** and some **bauxite** form by exactly this
  tropical weathering process.
