# 1.12 — The Geological Map of Nigeria

The map below ties the whole country together. It shows the **Basement Complex**
(the large crystalline areas), the **Younger Granites** (the Jos Plateau cluster),
and the **sedimentary basins** (Benue, Chad, Niger Delta, Dahomey, Sokoto).

![Geological map of Nigeria](../assets/images/map-geological-nigeria.png)

*Figure 1.12 — Geological map of Nigeria. Source: [IGRAC — Africa Groundwater Atlas (2019)](https://ggis.un-igrac.org/).*

## How to read it — the three domains in space

1. **Basement Complex** — the extensive crystalline regions of the **north,
   central, east and west**. Think **gold, tin, columbite, gems, marbles**.
2. **Younger Granites** — the cluster of intrusions on the **Jos Plateau**
   (central-east). Think **tin & columbite**.
3. **Sedimentary basins** — the large, relatively uniform areas:
   - the **Benue Trough** cutting NE–SW down the middle,
   - the **Chad Basin** in the northeast,
   - the **Niger Delta** along the southern coast,
   - the **Dahomey Basin** in the southwest,
   - the **Sokoto/Iullemmeden** in the northwest.

## A map-legend guide (what the units mean)

| Map unit | Rock type | What to expect |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Crystalline (basement) | Granite, gneiss, schist, migmatite | Gold, tin, columbite, gems, industrial |
| Younger Granite cluster | Ring-complex granites | Tin, columbite, tungsten, gems |
| Undifferentiated sedimentary | Sandstone, shale, limestone | Coal, oil/gas, salt, limestone, lead-zinc |
| Coastal/alluvial | Sands, muds, beach deposits | Placer heavy minerals, oil, sand |

## From map to provinces

The domains break into the **geological provinces** profiled in **Part IV**:
Jos Plateau tin province, the SW schist belts, the Benue Trough, the Chad and
Sokoto basins, the Niger Delta, the placer belts, and the Basement terranes — each
with its **states**.

## A quick mental compass

- **Coastal south & the delta** → **oil & gas**.
- **Middle of the country (Benue)** → **coal, lead-zinc, salt, limestone**.
- **Far northeast (Chad)** → **gypsum, salt, limestone, uranium**.
- **Jos Plateau (central-east)** → **tin, columbite, tungsten, gems**.
- **The crystalline halves (basement)** → **gold, pegmatite minerals, industrial
  minerals, water**.

## Using the map in practice

1. **Fix your licence/area to a domain** — it sets your target deposit types.
2. **Overlay lineaments** (from satellite imagery) on the geology to find structural
   intersections.
3. **Plan access** — rivers, roads, and terrain control where you can actually work.
4. **Pick sample sites** along structures and at domain boundaries (contacts).
5. **Note state boundaries** — cross-reference the **state-by-state table** (§4.9).

> **Field note.** The best modern map to carry is the **NGGS Geological Map of
> Nigeria**. The image here is a compact reference — for real fieldwork, obtain the
> official **1:1,000,000 (or 1:2,000,000)** NGGS map and current GSN memoirs.

## Tips & facts

- **Tip:** print the map and mark your traverse, sample points, and structures as
  you go — a marked-up map is a working exploration tool.
- **Fact:** the **Benue Trough** is the single most conspicuous feature — a NE–SW
  belt that splits the Basement in two.
- **Fact:** boundaries between domains (**contacts**) are often the most prospective
  ground — that's where different rock types and fluids meet.
