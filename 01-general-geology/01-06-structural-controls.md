# 1.10 — Structural Controls on Mineralization

Ore deposits rarely sit randomly — they follow **structure**: faults, fractures,
folds, contacts, and deep lineaments that acted as **plumbing for mineralizing
fluids**. In Nigeria, mapping structure is often the fastest route to targets.

## The main structural trends

Nigeria is crossed by several lineament systems, inherited from the Precambrian and
reactivated over time:

- **NE–SW** trends — e.g. the **Ifewara–Zungeru** lineament; the axis of the
  **Benue Trough**; part of the **Cameroon Line**.
- **E–W** trends — common in parts of the Basement.
- **NW–SE** trends — the dominant grain of many **schist belts**.

These show up on satellite images and aeromagnetic data as straight or gently
curving lines — often marking **faults and shear zones**.

| Trend | Where it's strongest | What it controls |
|-------|----------------------|------------------|
| **NE–SW** | Benue Trough; Ifewara–Zungeru | Basin axis; gold shears |
| **E–W** | Parts of the Basement | Fracture sets, some dikes |
| **NW–SE** | SW schist belts (Maru, Ilesa) | Gold-bearing shear zones |

## How structure makes ore

| Setting | What happens | Nigerian example |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **Shear zone / fault** | Fluids rise along the fracture, deposit minerals | **Gold** in the schist-belt shears (Maru, Ilesa) |
| **Ring fracture (granite)** | Rare-element fluids focus into ring dykes & pegmatites | **Tin & columbite** at the Jos ring complexes |
| **Contact** | Metamorphism/alteration at an intrusion's edge | **Skarn**-type mineralization, tungsten |
| **Fold hinge / anticline** | Traps fluids; in basins, traps oil | **Lead-zinc** veins; **oil** rollover anticlines |
| **Unconformity** | Redox/chemical traps | Some **uranium** occurrences |

### Why dilatant zones matter

Where a fault **bends, steps over, or splays**, it opens up (a **dilatant jog**).
Pressure drops and fluids boil/mix, so ore minerals precipitate there. This is why
the richest pockets sit at **fault bends and intersections**, not along straight
stretches — always sample the bends.

## Key structural targets to know

- **Schist-belt shear zones** → **gold** (SW Nigeria: Osun, Oyo, Kwara, Niger, Edo).
- **Ring-fracture/pegmatite systems of the Younger Granites** → **tin, columbite,
  tantalum, tungsten** (Jos Plateau).
- **Benue Trough faults & folds** → **lead-zinc veins** and **salt/limestone**
  (also the basin's oil traps).
- **The Cameroon Line** → control on the **Biu Plateau** volcanism.

## Mapping structure in the field

1. **Measure strike & dip** of beds, foliation, faults, and veins with a
   compass-clinometer.
2. **Plot them** on your base map/GPS — patterns emerge (e.g. a consistent shear set).
3. **Overlay on satellite imagery** (free Sentinel/Landsat) to extend lineaments
   beyond your traverse.
4. **Mark intersections and bends** — your highest-priority sample sites.
5. Note **alteration halos** (clay, silica, iron) along the structures.

## Worked examples

- **Gold (Maru/Ilesa shears):** map the shear zone → sample quartz veins & altered
  rock → assay **Au + As/Sb** → follow the anomaly along the shear.
- **Tin (Jos ring complexes):** map the ring dykes & pegmatite field → pan rivers →
  trace cassiterite upstream to the lode.
- **Lead-zinc (Abakaliki):** map gossans & calcite veins in the shales → channel
  sample → assay Pb, Zn, Ag.
- **Oil (Niger Delta):** seismic mapping of **growth faults** defines the rollover
  traps that are drilled.

> **Field note.** Always record the **orientation of veins and fractures**, the
> **foliation**, and any **alteration halo**. A mineralized sample next to a shear
> is a far better target than the same sample in unremarkable ground.

## Tips & facts

- **Tip:** ore follows **structure** — when in doubt, map fractures and shears first.
- **Tip:** a rusty/clay-rich fault zone with quartz veins is the classic surface
  signature of the "plumbing."
- **Fact:** many Nigerian gold mines sit directly on **schist-belt shear zones** —
  structure is the control, not just rock type.
- **Fact:** the Jos tin was focused by **ring fractures** of the Younger Granite
  complexes — a magmatic structural control.
