# 1.1–1.2 — Overview & the Three Great Domains

## Where Nigeria sits

Nigeria occupies about **923,800 km²** of West Africa, between roughly **4°–14°N**
and **2°–15°E**. It is bounded by **Benin** (west), **Niger** (north), **Chad**
(northeast), **Cameroon** (east), and the **Gulf of Guinea / Atlantic Ocean**
(south). This position places it across the junction of the ancient **West African
Craton** and younger belts and basins.

## Nigeria at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Area** | ~923,800 km² |
| **Latitude / Longitude** | ~4°–14°N · 2°–15°E |
| **Neighbours** | Benin, Niger, Chad, Cameroon; Gulf of Guinea (south) |
| **Population** | Africa's largest (>200 million) |
| **Climate** | Tropical; wet south (Niger Delta, >2,000 mm rain) to Sahelian north (<600 mm) |
| **Relief** | Coastal plains → low plateaus → highlands (Jos, Mambilla, Obudu) |
| **Geologic setting** | Western **West African Craton** + Pan-African belts + sedimentary basins |

> **Why climate matters to a geologist:** heavy tropical rain and warm temperatures
> drive **deep weathering** — thick laterite, placers, and hidden ore — which shapes
> how you explore (geochemistry and geophysics, not just outcrop).

## The big picture: three domains

Almost everything in Nigerian geology falls into **one of three domains**. Learn
this division and the whole country makes sense:

| Domain | Age | What it is | Key minerals |
|--------|-----|-----------|--------------|
| **1. Basement Complex** | Precambrian (~3.6 Ga–~600 Ma) | Ancient crystalline metamorphic & igneous rocks | **Gold**, tin, columbite, tantalum, gemstones, marble |
| **2. Younger Granites** | Jurassic (~200–140 Ma) | Ring-complex granites, mostly on the **Jos Plateau** | **Tin, columbite-tantalite, tungsten**, gems |
| **3. Sedimentary Basins** | Cretaceous–Recent | Flat-lying basins | **Oil & gas**, coal, lead-zinc, salt, limestone, gypsum |

```
        BASEMENT COMPLEX          (northern, central, eastern, western halves)
   +-----------------------------------+
   |   [YOUNGER GRANITES]  ~ Jos       |
   |                                    |
   |        ~~~~ BENUE TROUGH ~~~~      |  (a NE-SW rift splitting the basement)
   |                                    |
   +-----------------------------------+
        SEDIMENTARY BASINS      (Benue · Chad · Niger Delta · Dahomey · Sokoto)
```

## Domain 1 — the Basement Complex (in brief)

Ancient **Precambrian** crystalline rock under ~half the country. Migmatites,
gneisses, schists, quartzites, and intrusive granites, sliced by **schist belts**
that host **gold**. Full detail in **§1.3–1.4**.

## Domain 2 — the Younger Granites (in brief)

**Jurassic** alkaline ring complexes (Jos Plateau and environs) — Nigeria's great
**tin–columbite–tungsten** and gem province. Full detail in **§1.5**.

## Domain 3 — the Sedimentary Basins (in brief)

Five basins (**Benue, Chad, Niger Delta, Dahomey, Sokoto**) holding the **oil, gas,
coal, salt, limestone, lead-zinc, and gypsum**. Full detail in **§1.6–1.8**.

## Why the three-domain picture matters to you

| If you're standing on… | You're in… | Think… |
|------------------------|-----------|--------|
| Hard crystalline rock (granite, gneiss, schist, quartzite) | **Basement / Younger Granites** | gold, tin, columbite, gems, mica, talc |
| Coarse pegmatite veins | Basement/Younger Granite margins | cassiterite, columbite, lithium, gems |
| Flat-lying sandstone/shale/limestone | **Sedimentary basin** | oil, gas, coal, salt, limestone, lead-zinc |
| Dark fine basalt cap | Young volcanics (Jos/Biu) | aggregate; check underlying target |

> **Field note.** This one distinction — *crystalline basement* vs. *sedimentary
> basin* — will guide your expectations more than any other single rule in Nigeria.

## From domains to provinces

The three domains map onto the **geological provinces** detailed in **Part IV**:
the Jos Plateau tin province, the SW schist belts, the Benue Trough, the Chad and
Sokoto basins, the Niger Delta, and the Basement terranes — each profiled with its
**states**.

## A word on how Nigeria is often subdivided

Geologists also speak of the **Basement Complex** being divided into a northern and
southern half by the **Benue Trough**, and of named **schist belts** (e.g.
Ilesa–Ife, Egbe–Isanlu, Igarra, Maru) where gold and other minerals concentrate.
These are unpacked in the next notes and mapped in **§1.12**.

## Facts & tips

- **Fact:** the same 4°–14°N belt that gives Nigeria fertile land and heavy rain
  also gives it the intense weathering that forms its laterites and placers.
- **Fact:** Nigeria straddles the **West African Craton** — one of Earth's oldest
  and most stable continental cores — which is why its Basement is so old.
- **Tip:** before any fieldwork, fix your area to a **domain** using the NGGS map
  (§1.12); it tells you which deposit types to target.
- **Tip:** expect **regolith cover** across most of the Basement — plan geochemical
  sampling, not just outcrop mapping.
