# 0.6 — Structural Geology Basics

**Structure** is how rocks are arranged and deformed. In Nigeria, structures don't
just describe the rocks — they **control where minerals are**. Learn to read them.

## Measuring rock orientation: strike & dip

- **Strike** — the compass direction of a horizontal line on a tilted surface (a
  bedding plane, fault, or foliation).
- **Dip** — the angle and direction the surface slopes downward.

Recorded together as *strike/dip* (e.g. 030°/35° SE), usually with a compass-clinometer.

**How to measure (compass-clinometer):**
1. Hold the clinometer flat against the plane (bed, foliation, or fault surface).
2. Read the **dip direction** (the compass bearing down the slope).
3. Read the **dip angle** from the clinometer level.
4. **Strike** is 90° from the dip direction (e.g. dip 120°/40° → strike 030°).

## Folds

Bends in layered rock from compression. Key parts:

- **Anticline** — arches up; **syncline** — bends down.
- **Limb** — the flank of a fold; **hinge** — the bend; **axis** — the line of greatest bend.
- **Plunging folds** tilt their axis — their map pattern looks like a "U" or horseshoe.
- Classified by tightness: **gentle → open → tight → isoclinal** (interlimb angle 180°→~0°).

In Nigeria, folds are clearest in the **Benue Trough** and other sedimentary basins.
Fold **hinges and limbs** can focus fluids — the crest and flanks of anticlines are
classic traps for oil and for vein mineralization.

## Faults

Fractures along which rock has moved. Three main types:

| Type | Motion | Stress | Nigerian context |
|------|--------|--------|------------------|
| **Normal** | Hanging wall drops | Extension (pulling apart) | Rifts, Benue Trough margins, Niger Delta growth faults |
| **Reverse/thrust** | Hanging wall rises | Compression (squeezing) | Pan-African thrust belts |
| **Strike-slip** | Horizontal sliding | Shear | Major lineaments, shear zones |

- **Joint** — a fracture with **no** movement (still important: joints let fluids in,
  control groundwater, and guide weathering).
- **Fault rocks:** **breccia/gouge** (brittle, angular fragments) and **mylonite**
  (ductile, fine-grained, slickensided) — see [Mylonite & Cataclasite](../02-rocks-of-nigeria/metamorphic/mylonite-cataclasite.md).

## Foliation & lineation

- **Foliation** — planar alignment of minerals (flattened from pressure) → slate,
  schist, gneiss. It tells you the *direction of squeezing*.
- **Lineation** — linear alignment of minerals → tells the *direction of stretching*.
- **Axial-planar foliation** forms parallel to a fold's axial plane and is used to
  map large folds.

## Stress & strain (a quick note)

**Stress** is the force applied; **strain** is the resulting deformation. Rocks respond
differently at different depths: **brittle** (faults, joints) near the surface where
it's cool, and **ductile** (folds, foliation) deeper where it's hot and confining
pressure is high. That's why the deep Basement is folded while shallow basin rocks fault.

## Nigerian lineaments — the controls on ore

Nigeria is criss-crossed by ancient **lineaments** (linear features seen on
satellite/aeromagnetic data) and **shear zones** — deep fractures that acted as
**plumbing for mineralizing fluids**. Classic examples:

- The **Ifewara–Zungeru** and related NE–SW shear zones.
- The **Anka**, **Maru**, **Ilesa**, and other schist-belt shear zones that host **gold**.
- The ring-fracture systems of the **Younger Granites** that focus **tin/columbite**.
- The **Kurfa** and other lineaments that localize artisanal gold in the northwest.

## How structure controls ore (the key idea)

Ore fluids move along the **easiest pathways** and precipitate where conditions change:

1. **Dilatant jogs & bends** in faults — where a fault bends, it opens up and fills with quartz + ore.
2. **Fault/fold intersections** — crossing structures are prime ore sites.
3. **Fold hinges** — fractures concentrate at the bend (e.g. saddle-reef gold).
4. **Igneous contacts** — skarns and replacement ores form where magma meets reactive rock.
5. **Chemical traps** — ore drops out where fluids meet a reducing/reactive wall-rock (e.g. carbonaceous shale).

> **Field note.** When you map, always note where your mineralized samples sit
> relative to faults, folds, and foliation. Ore loves **structure** — a contact, a
> shear, a fracture set. This is the single biggest clue in Nigerian exploration.

## Tips & facts

- **Tip:** always measure and record **strike and dip** — it turns a sketch map into
  data and lets you project structures underground.
- **Tip:** rusty, clay-rich **fault zones** and **quartz veins** are the surface
  expression of the plumbing — sample them.
- **Fact:** many Nigerian gold mines sit exactly on **shear zones** within the schist
  belts — structure, not rock type alone, is the control.
- **Fact:** the Niger Delta's **growth faults** created the rollover traps that hold
  its oil and gas.
