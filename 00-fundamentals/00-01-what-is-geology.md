# 0.1 — What Geology Is (and Why It Matters in Nigeria)

## Definition

**Geology** (Greek *gē*, "Earth" + *-logia*, "study") is the science of the solid
Earth — its **materials**, its **structure**, the **processes** that act on it, and
its **history** through ~4.6 billion years. As an *exploration* geologist your job
is to read the rocks and find where useful minerals and energy have concentrated.

> **In one line:** geology is detective work — the rocks are the evidence, and an
> ore body is the culprit you're trying to locate.

## The four questions geologists ask

1. **What is it made of?** — minerals and rocks. *(Nigerian example: is that heavy,
   black grain cassiterite, columbite, or magnetite?)*
2. **How is it arranged?** — structure: layers, folds, faults, intrusions.
   *(Does the gold sit on a shear zone? Is the coal a flat seam or folded?)*
3. **How did it get that way?** — processes: magmatism, erosion, metamorphism,
   tectonics. *(Did the Younger Granites bring the tin, or did a river concentrate it?)*
4. **When did it happen?** — geologic time and dating. *(Is this Pan-African Basement
   or Jurassic granite? The age tells you what to expect.)*

## The geologist's field toolkit

| Tool | Use |
|------|-----|
| **10× hand lens** | Identify minerals, check grain size and texture |
| **Geological hammer** | Break rock, test hardness, make fresh surfaces |
| **Compass clinometer** | Measure the orientation of beds, foliation, faults, veins |
| **GPS / phone** | Fix every observation and sample to a location |
| **Streak plate** (unglazed porcelain) | Powder colour of a mineral |
| **Dilute HCl (10%)** | Test for carbonate (fizz) |
| **Hand magnet** | Detect magnetite and pyrrhotite |
| **Field notebook + sample bags/tags** | Record lithology, structure, alteration, sample numbers |
| **Maps (NGGS) & satellite imagery** | Regional geology, lineaments, access |

## Why geology matters to Nigeria specifically

Nigeria's economy leans heavily on geology. As a field geologist you will touch:

- **Petroleum & gas** — the Niger Delta is Africa's biggest oil province; oil and gas
  dominate export earnings.
- **Solid minerals** — tin (Jos Plateau), gold (Zamfara, Osun, Kebbi…), columbite-
  tantalite, iron ore (Itakpe), lead–zinc (Abakaliki), limestone, kaolin, and more
  (detailed in **Part III**).
- **Construction materials** — granite, sand, gravel, and limestone for cement — the
  largest solid-mineral sector by volume.
- **Water** — groundwater in the Basement fractures and in the sedimentary basins;
  a hydrogeologist's problem is a geologist's problem.
- **Hazards** — earth tremors, landslides, flooding, erosion, and mine-safety issues.

## The three great domains (the big picture)

Almost all of Nigeria's rocks fall into three domains — memorize this:

1. **The Basement Complex** (~half the country) — ancient Precambrian crystalline
   rocks (granites, gneisses, schists). Hosts **gold, tin, columbite, gems, and
   industrial minerals**.
2. **The Younger Granites** (Jurassic ring complexes, mainly the Jos Plateau) —
   intruded the Basement and carry the famous **tin–columbite–tungsten** and gem
   mineralization.
3. **The Sedimentary Basins** (Benue Trough, Chad/Borno, Niger Delta, Sokoto,
   Dahomey) — hold **coal, oil & gas, limestone, salt, gypsum, lead–zinc, and
   bitumen**.

> **Field note.** Keep that three-part picture in your head; almost everything else
> hangs off it (covered in **Part I**). Knowing which domain you're standing in
> tells you what you're likely to find.

## Branches of geology (as applied here)

| Branch | Deals with | What you actually do | Nigerian relevance |
|--------|------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Mineralogy | Minerals | ID ores/gems by hand tests | Identifying ores, gems, industrial minerals |
| Petrology | Rocks | Describe & classify rocks in the field | Mapping and understanding ore hosts |
| Structural geology | Deformation | Measure foliation, folds, faults, lineaments | Faults/lineaments that control mineralization |
| Stratigraphy & sedimentology | Rock layers | Log sections, correlate units | Coal, limestone, oil reservoirs |
| Economic geology | Ore deposits | Generate and rank targets | The core of exploration |
| Geochemistry | Element distribution | Soil/stream sampling & assays | Soil/stream geochemical surveys |
| Geophysics | Physical properties | Gravity, magnetics, seismic, resistivity | Map structure & depth to bedrock |
| Hydrogeology | Groundwater | Site boreholes, manage mine water | Water supply, mine dewatering |
| Environmental geology | Human impacts | Plan reclamation, control AMD | Reclamation, tailings, acid mine drainage |

## The exploration mindset

Exploration moves from **regional → target → prospect → deposit**, narrowing ground
at each step using evidence:

1. **Regional** — desk study, NGGS maps, airborne geophysics over a large area.
2. **Target** — define favourable ground (a schist belt, a ring complex, a structure).
3. **Prospect** — soil/rock geochemistry, detailed geophysics, trenching, pitting.
4. **Deposit** — drilling, sampling, assay, resource estimation.

> Most exploration budgets are spent proving a target *wrong* as cheaply as possible.
> Spend small money early; save the drill for the best ground.

## Facts & tips

- **Fact:** Nigeria sits on the West African Craton; much of the Basement was stitched
  together during the **Pan-African Orogeny (~600 million years ago)**.
- **Fact:** the Jos Plateau **tin** boom began in the early 1900s and made Nigeria a
  world-class tin producer for decades.
- **Fact:** Nigeria is a top African **oil** producer, yet its **solid-minerals**
  sector remains underdeveloped relative to its potential — that gap is your opportunity.
- **Tip:** every observation needs three things — a **location (GPS)**, a **photo with
  scale**, and a **note**. An unlocated sample is worthless.
- **Tip:** in Nigeria's deep tropical weathering, surface rock is often hidden under
  laterite — expect to use geochemistry and geophysics, not just outcrop mapping.
