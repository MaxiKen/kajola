# 0.4 — The Three Rock Families

Every rock belongs to one of three families, defined by **how it formed**. In
Nigeria you will meet all three — and the family a rock belongs to immediately
tells you what minerals and ores to expect.

## Igneous rocks — "fire rocks"

Formed from **cooled magma or lava**. Classified by **texture** (crystal size, set by
cooling rate) and **composition** (minerals present).

- **Intrusive (plutonic)** — magma cools slowly underground → **coarse-grained**
  (visible crystals). *E.g. granite, diorite, gabbro, pegmatite.*
- **Extrusive (volcanic)** — lava cools fast at the surface → **fine-grained** or
  glassy. *E.g. basalt, rhyolite, tuff, obsidian.*
- **Porphyritic** — large crystals (phenocrysts) in a fine matrix — a two-stage
  cooling history.

**Composition controls the minerals (and the ores):**

| Composition | Minerals | Typical rock | Ores to expect |
|-------------|----------|--------------|----------------|
| Felsic (silica-rich) | Quartz, K-feldspar, mica | Granite, rhyolite | Tin, tungsten, columbite, gems, kaolin |
| Intermediate | Plagioclase, amphibole | Diorite, andesite | Copper, gold, porphyry-style |
| Mafic | Pyroxene, olivine, Ca-plagioclase | Gabbro, basalt | Nickel, chromium, titanium |
| Ultramafic | Olivine, pyroxene | Peridotite | Chromite, nickel, platinum-group, asbestos |

**In Nigeria:** the **Older Granites** and the Jurassic **Younger Granites** (ring
complexes of the Jos Plateau) — the latter host the famous **tin–columbite**
mineralization (see **Parts II & III**).

## Sedimentary rocks — "layered rocks"

Formed at the surface from **deposited sediment** that is compacted and cemented
(**lithified**). Three kinds:

- **Clastic** — from rock fragments, graded by grain size:
  **conglomerate → sandstone → siltstone → shale** (coarse → fine).
- **Chemical** — precipitated from water: **limestone, dolomite, gypsum, rock salt,
  travertine**.
- **Organic** — from organisms: **coal, chalk, fossiliferous limestone**.

![Sandstone (clastic sedimentary)](../assets/images/rock-sandstone.jpg)
*Figure 0.4b — Sandstone. Source: [Dreamstime](https://dreamstime.com/photos-images/sedimentary-rock.html).*

**In Nigeria:** the **Benue Trough** (coal at Enugu, lead-zinc, limestone, salt),
the **Chad Basin** (gypsum, salt), and the **Niger Delta** (sand, shale — and the
oil reservoirs). See **Parts I & II**.

## Metamorphic rocks — "changed rocks"

Formed when existing rock is transformed by **heat, pressure, and/or fluids**
**without melting**. Two key distinctions:

- **Foliated** — minerals aligned in bands/planes. A **grade** sequence runs
  **slate → phyllite → schist → gneiss** (low → high grade; bigger crystals, stronger
  banding).
- **Non-foliated** — no alignment: **marble** (from limestone), **quartzite** (from
  sandstone), **hornfels** (contact-baked shale).

![Gneiss (foliated metamorphic)](../assets/images/rock-gneiss.webp)
*Figure 0.4c — Gneiss. Source: [Geoscopy](https://geoscopy.com/rock/gneiss/).*

**In Nigeria:** the **Basement Complex** is largely metamorphic — migmatites,
gneisses, quartzites, and the **schist belts** of the southwest (important for
**gold**). See **Parts II & IV**.

## Which family → which resources

| Family | Signature Nigerian resources |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Igneous | Tin, columbite, tungsten, gold, gems, kaolin, granite, chromite/nickel (ultramafics) |
| Sedimentary | Coal, oil & gas, limestone, salt, gypsum, lead-zinc, bitumen, sand, clay |
| Metamorphic | Gold (schist belts), marble, kyanite/sillimanite, talc, gem garnet |

## Lookalike distinctions (field quick-checks)

| Confusion | How to tell |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Granite vs gneiss** | Gneiss has **foliation/banding**; granite is massive with randomly oriented crystals |
| **Quartzite vs sandstone** | Quartzite grains are **interlocked** (breaks through grains); sandstone breaks around grains (gritty) |
| **Marble vs limestone** | Marble has **interlocking calcite crystals** (sugary, no fossils); limestone keeps fossils/grains |
| **Slate vs shale** | Slate has **slaty cleavage** (splits into thin flat sheets even at angles to bedding) |
| **Schist vs gneiss** | Schist: minerals (mica) all aligned; gneiss: alternating light/dark **bands** |

## Tips & facts

- **Tip:** the *texture* tells you the cooling/deposition/grade history; the
  *composition* tells you the minerals. Use both to name the rock.
- **Fact:** one parent rock can make several metamorphic rocks — shale becomes
  **slate → phyllite → schist**, or **hornfels/skarn** near a hot intrusion.
- **Fact:** the grain-size ladder (conglomerate → sandstone → siltstone → shale)
  records **decreasing energy** of the depositing current — a river's energy history
  written in stone.
- **Fact:** Nigeria's three great domains map neatly onto families — Basement
  (igneous + metamorphic), Younger Granites (igneous), and Sedimentary Basins
  (sedimentary).

## Quick comparison

| Family | Formed by | Texture cue | Nigerian example |
|--------|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| Igneous | Cooled magma/lava | Interlocking crystals | Granite (Younger Granites, Jos) |
| Sedimentary | Deposited & cemented sediment | Layers/grains | Sandstone & limestone (Benue Trough) |
| Metamorphic | Heat & pressure (no melting) | Foliation/bands | Gneiss & quartzite (Basement) |
