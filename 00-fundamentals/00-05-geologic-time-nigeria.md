# 0.5 — Geologic Time & the Nigerian Stratigraphic Column

Geology is a **historical** science. To read Nigeria's rocks you must place them on
a timeline spanning ~4.6 billion years.

## Two ways to date

### Relative dating (order, not numbers)
- **Superposition** — in undisturbed layers, oldest is at the bottom.
- **Original horizontality** — sediments are deposited flat; tilted beds were moved later.
- **Cross-cutting relationships** — a feature that cuts another is **younger**
  (a fault or intrusion is younger than what it cuts).
- **Inclusions** — a fragment included in a rock is older than the rock.
- **Fossil succession** — life has changed through time, so fossils date and correlate layers.

### Absolute (radiometric) dating (numbers, in years)
Measures the decay of radioactive **parent** isotopes to stable **daughter** products.
Because decay is constant, the parent:daughter ratio is a clock (each isotope has a
**half-life** — the time for half the parent to decay).

| Method | Parent → Daughter | Half-life | Best for dating |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **U–Pb** | Uranium → Lead | ~0.7–4.5 Ga | Zircon, monazite, uraninite — **granites, pegmatites** |
| **K–Ar / Ar–Ar** | Potassium → Argon | 1.25 Ga | Micas, feldspars, **basalts** |
| **Rb–Sr** | Rubidium → Strontium | 49 Ga | Old metamorphic/igneous rocks |
| **C–14** | Carbon → Nitrogen | 5,730 yr | Organic material < ~50,000 yr (peat, recent shells) |

> **Field note.** A geologist writes ages as **Ma** (mega-annum = million years ago)
> or **Ga** (giga-annum = billion years ago). "600 Ma" = 600 million years ago.

## The geological timescale (essentials)

From oldest to youngest: **Precambrian** (Hadean → Archean → Proterozoic) →
**Phanerozoic** (Paleozoic → Mesozoic → Cenozoic). The Precambrian covers the first
~4 billion years; abundant complex life appears only in the Phanerozoic.

| Eon | Era | Period | Age (approx.) | Nigerian rock record |
|-----|-----|--------|---------------|----------------------|
| Precambrian | Archean–Proterozoic | — | 4600–541 Ma | **Basement Complex** gneisses, migmatites, schist belts |
| Phanerozoic | Paleozoic | Cambrian–Permian | 541–252 Ma | Largely absent/unconformity (Nigeria was being eroded or not yet deposited) |
| Phanerozoic | Mesozoic | Triassic–Jurassic | 252–145 Ma | **Younger Granites** ring complexes (Jos) |
| Phanerozoic | Mesozoic | Cretaceous | 145–66 Ma | **Benue & Chad** basin fill; rifting; Bima Sandstone, coal, limestone |
| Phanerozoic | Cenozoic | Paleogene–Neogene | 66–2.6 Ma | **Niger Delta** growth; Jos & Biu **basalts** |
| Phanerozoic | Cenozoic | Quaternary | 2.6 Ma–now | Alluvium, beach sands, laterite, active Niger Delta |

## The Nigerian stratigraphic column

Most of Nigeria's story is **Precambrian to Cenozoic**:

| Age | What formed | Where |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| **Archean–Early Proterozoic** (~3.6–2.0 Ga) | Ancient **Basement Complex** gneisses, migmatites | Core of the country |
| **Mid–Late Proterozoic** | More basement; schist belts | Widely |
| **Pan-African Orogeny** (~600 Ma) | Granitoids, metamorphism, deformation | Basement reworked |
| **Jurassic** (~200–140 Ma) | **Younger Granites** (ring complexes) | Jos Plateau & environs |
| **Cretaceous** (~145–66 Ma) | **Benue & Chad** basin sediments; rifting | Benue Trough, Borno |
| **Cenozoic** (66 Ma–now) | **Niger Delta** sediments; Jos & Biu **basalts**; Chad & Dahomey fill | Niger Delta, NE, SW |

```
Oldest ───────────────────────────────────────────────► Youngest
Basement   Pan-African   Younger     Cretaceous   Niger Delta
Complex    (~600 Ma)     Granites    basins       + basalts
(~2–3.6 Ga)              (~150 Ma)   (~100 Ma)    (0–66 Ma)
```

> **Field note.** A rule of thumb: if you're standing on **hard crystalline rock**
> (granite, gneiss, schist) you're on the **Basement** — roughly the northern,
> central, eastern and western halves. If you're on **flat-lying sedimentary rock**
> (sandstone, limestone, shale, coal) you're in one of the **basins** — the middle
> (Benue), northeast (Chad), or the coastal south (Niger Delta/Dahomey). See **Part I**.

## Why the column matters for exploration

- The **Younger Granites** (Jurassic) are the tin/columbite/tantalum factory.
- The **Benue Trough** sediments carry the coal, lead-zinc, salt, and ironstone.
- The **Niger Delta** Cenozoic sediments are the oil & gas reservoirs.
- The **Basement schist belts** are the gold targets.

## Tips & facts

- **Fact:** the Precambrian makes up ~88% of Earth's history, yet has few fossils —
  so Nigerian Basement rocks are dated mainly by **radiometric** methods, not fossils.
- **Fact:** radiometric dates on **zircon and monazite** from pegmatites are how the
  Younger Granites' age (~200–140 Ma) was pinned down.
- **Tip:** fossils (foraminifera, pollen) date the sedimentary basins and guide oil,
  gas, and coal correlation.
- **Fact:** Nigeria's youngest rocks — Niger Delta muds and coastal beach sands — are
  still being deposited today, closing the rock cycle in front of us.
- **Tip:** an **unconformity** (a buried erosion surface) marks a gap in the record —
  in Nigeria, the great unconformity between Basement and basin fill is a classic
  example.
