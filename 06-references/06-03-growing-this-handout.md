# 6.3 — Keeping This Handout Growing

This handout is a **living field document**. Here's how to extend it consistently.

## Profile templates (recap)
Every rock and mineral follows the same structure so you can compare specimens quickly.

**Rock profile** → Composition · Texture · Diagnostic field features · Host
states/localities · Associated minerals · Economic relevance · Identification notes ·
Images (hand specimen + in situ).

**Mineral profile** → Chemistry & mineralogy · Crystal habit · Physical properties (and
how to test) · Occurrence with images (pure + in rock/ore) · Genesis · States/localities ·
Mining, uses & market · Exploration notes.

## Image conventions
- **Real photographs only** — web-sourced specimens, with a **source credit** in the
  caption. No AI-generated images.
- **Two-image rule** for minerals: a **pure/hand-specimen** image *and* an
  **in-rock/ore occurrence** image. (For massive igneous rocks and bulk industrial
  materials, one hand-specimen image is acceptable since pure ≈ in-rock.)
- Store images in `assets/images/` and reference them with a **relative path**.
- Give every image a short caption *with its source*.

## Adding a new rock or mineral
1. Create a file in the right folder (`02-rocks-of-nigeria/…`, `03-mineral-resources/…`).
2. Use the profile template above.
3. Add two credited images (minerals) or one (massive/bulk).
4. Link it from the relevant **README** table and from related profiles.
5. Run a **link check** so nothing breaks.

## Citation style
- Inline links to related profiles and the sources listed in **[§6.1](06-01-key-references.md)**
  and **[§6.2](06-02-online-resources-maps.md)**.
- Always cross-check localities against the current **NGGS** map and **GSN/company**
  reports before relying on them in the field.

## A closing note
Geology is learned in the **field**, not just on the page. Use this handout to know what
to *look for* — then go and look, describe, sample, and record. Keep your own notes, add
your photos, and let the handout grow with your experience.

> *"The rock doesn't lie — but it rewards the prepared eye."*
