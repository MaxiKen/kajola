# Limestone & Dolomite

> **Family:** Sedimentary — chemical/organic · **Composition:** calcite (CaCO₃) ± dolomite

## Composition
Chiefly **calcite** (limestone) or **dolomite** CaMg(CO₃)₂ (dolostone), from
accumulated shells, corals, chemical precipitates, or oolites.

## Texture
**Crystalline to granular**; may be **fossiliferous** (full of shells/corals) or
**oolitic** (tiny spherical grains).

## Diagnostic field features
- **Fizzes vigorously with dilute hydrochloric acid** — the key field test.
- Usually **light grey/white/cream**; scratched easily by a **steel knife** (hardness ~3).
- Often contains **fossils** (shells, corals).

## Host states / localities
Widely mined for cement: **Ewekoro & Shagamu (Ogun), Nkalagu (Enugu), Gboko (Benue),
Igalamela (Kogi), Calabar (Cross River), Sokoto, Maiduguri (Borno)**, and more.

## Associated minerals
Calcite, dolomite, quartz, chert, clay; may host **lead-zinc** (MVT-type) and
**barite** where mineralized.

## Economic relevance
- **CEMENT** — one of Nigeria's most important industrial minerals (many cement
  plants run on these deposits).
- **Lime, flux** (steel), **aggregate**, and **building/dimension stone**.
- Source of **calcium carbonate** for many industries.

## Identification notes
- **Acid fizz + soft (knife-scratchable)** = limestone/dolomite.
- **vs. marble:** marble also fizzes but is coarser, harder, and metamorphic
  (recrystallized).
- **vs. sandstone:** sandstone is gritty and usually doesn't fizz.
