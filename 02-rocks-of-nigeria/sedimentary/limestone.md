# Limestone & Dolomite

> **Family:** Sedimentary — chemical/organic · **Composition:** calcite (CaCO₃) ± dolomite CaMg(CO₃)₂
> **Quick ID:** Fizzes vigorously with dilute acid + soft (knife-scratchable) + often fossiliferous.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Light grey, white, cream (sometimes dark if impure) |
| Composition | Calcite (limestone) or dolomite (dolostone) |
| Hardness | ~3 (soft — scratched by a steel knife) |
| Acid test | **Fizzes vigorously** with dilute HCl |
| Texture | Crystalline to granular; fossiliferous or oolitic |
| Setting | Basins & Basement carbonate lenses |

## Composition
Chiefly **calcite** (limestone) or **dolomite CaMg(CO₃)₂** (dolostone), from accumulated shells, corals, chemical precipitates, or oolites. Impurities (clay, silica, iron) give colour variations; **chert** nodules are common.

## Origin & classification
Limestone is a **chemical/organic sedimentary rock** — it forms from the accumulation of **biological material** (shells, corals, algae) or **chemical precipitation** of calcium carbonate in shallow seas and lakes. Varieties: **fossiliferous** (full of shells), **oolitic** (tiny spherical ooids), **chalk** (soft, microscopic), and **dolostone** (dolomite-rich).

## Texture & sedimentary structures
**Crystalline to granular**; may be **fossiliferous** (full of shells/corals) or **oolitic** (tiny spherical grains). Often **bedded**; may show **cross-bedding** and **reef structures**. Dolostone is typically more crystalline and sugary than limestone.

## Diagnostic field features
- **Fizzes vigorously with dilute hydrochloric acid** — the key field test.
- Usually **light grey/white/cream**; scratched easily by a **steel knife** (hardness ~3).
- Often contains **fossils** (shells, corals).
- **Dolomite** fizzes only weakly (needs powdered/stronger acid).

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Acid:** fizzes vigorously with dilute HCl (calcite limestone); dolomite fizzes weakly.
2. **Hardness:** soft — a knife scratches it easily (unlike quartzite).
3. **Fossils:** look for shells, corals, or ooids.
4. **Vs marble:** marble is coarser, harder, metamorphic (recrystallised); limestone is softer, often fossiliferous.
5. **Vs sandstone:** sandstone is gritty and doesn't fizz.

## Depositional setting & formation
Limestone forms in warm, shallow, clear **marine** (or sometimes lake) settings where calcium carbonate accumulates from shells, corals, and chemical precipitation. Nigeria's limestones formed in the ancient seas of the Benue Trough, the Dak/Sokoto basins, and as lenses in the Basement.

## Host states / localities
Widely mined for cement:
- **Ewekoro & Shagamu** (Ogun)
- **Nkalagu** (Enugu), **Gboko** (Benue)
- **Igalamela** (Kogi), **Calabar** (Cross River)
- **Sokoto**, **Maiduguri** (Borno), and more.

## Associated minerals & rocks
Calcite, dolomite, quartz, chert, clay; may host **lead-zinc** (MVT-type) and **barite** where mineralised. Associated rocks: shale, sandstone, marble (metamorphosed equivalent), gypsum (evaporite).

## Economic relevance
- **CEMENT** — one of Nigeria's most important industrial minerals; many cement plants run on these deposits (Ewekoro, Nkalagu, etc.).
- **Lime, flux** (steelmaking), **aggregate**, and **building/dimension stone**.
- Source of **calcium carbonate** for many industries (glass, paper, chemicals).
- Host for **lead–zinc** (MVT) and **barite** mineralisation.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Marble** | Marble also fizzes but is coarser, harder, and metamorphic (recrystallised); limestone is softer, often fossiliferous. |
| **Dolostone** | Dolostone fizzes only weakly (needs powdered sample/stronger acid); limestone fizzes vigorously. |
| **Sandstone** | Sandstone is gritty and usually doesn't fizz. |
| **Chalk** | Chalk is very soft, white, and fine (microscopic shells). |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Fizzes vigorously with acid?
- [ ] Soft (knife-scratchable)?
- [ ] Fossils / ooids present?
- [ ] Light grey/white/cream?
- [ ] Weak fizz (→ dolomite)?

## Field tips
- **Acid fizz + soft (knife-scratchable)** = limestone/dolomite.
- **vs. marble:** marble also fizzes but is coarser, harder, and metamorphic (recrystallised).
- **vs. sandstone:** sandstone is gritty and usually doesn't fizz.
- Carry **dilute HCl** — the acid test is the definitive limestone ID.
- Note **fossils and bedding** — they reveal the depositional environment.

## Facts
- Limestone is the raw material for **cement** — Nigeria has many cement plants on these deposits.
- **Ewekoro (Ogun)** and **Nkalagu (Enugu)** are among Nigeria's largest limestone quarries.
- Limestone **fizzes** because it is **calcium carbonate** (the same chemistry as chalk and seashells).
- Much limestone is **biological** — built from accumulated shells and corals.
