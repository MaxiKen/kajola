# Rock Salt (Halite)

> **Family:** Sedimentary — chemical (evaporite) · **Composition:** sodium chloride (NaCl)

## Composition
**Halite (NaCl)** — common salt — deposited by the evaporation of saline water
(seawater or brines) in restricted basins and lakes.

## Texture
**Crystalline**, often in **cubic crystals** or granular beds; clear, white, grey,
or pink; **soluble in water**.

## Diagnostic field features
- **Salty taste** — the definitive test (taste a fresh surface).
- **Cubic cleavage** — breaks into small cubes.
- **Soft** (hardness ~2.5) and **dissolves** readily in water.

## Host states / localities
- **Lake Chad** (Borno) — traditional salt-producing area.
- **Awe** (Nasarawa) — rock-salt deposits.
- **Ijero** (Ekiti) — rock salt.
- Evaporite beds in the **Chad Basin** and elsewhere.

## Associated minerals
Gypsum, anhydrite, sylvite (potash), other evaporite minerals.

## Economic relevance
- **SALT** — edible salt, industrial chemicals (chlor-alkali), water treatment, and
  a source of **sodium and chlorine**.
- Lake Chad salt supports local economies.

## Identification notes
- **Salty taste + cubic break + soft + dissolves** = halite (rock salt).
- Distinguished from gypsum by **taste** (salty vs. not) and from calcite by
  **solubility and lack of fizz**.
