# Anhydrite

## Composition
**Anhydrite, CaSO₄** — the anhydrous (water-free) form of gypsum; an **evaporite**
mineral that forms rock beds with gypsum and halite.

## Texture
Crystalline, massive, fibrous, or nodular.

## Diagnostic field features
- Hardness ~3½ (harder than gypsum); **no water of crystallization**.
- **Hydrates to gypsum** on exposure to water (expands); lacks the perfect cleavage and
  softness of gypsum.

## Host states / localities
Occurs in evaporite sequences with gypsum and halite — the **Chad (Borno/Yobe) Basin**,
and the Benue/Nasarawa salt–gypsum areas
(see [Gypsum & Evaporites](gypsum-evaporites.md), [Rock Salt (Halite)](rock-salt.md)).

## Associated minerals
Gypsum, halite, calcite, dolomite, polyhalite (evaporite minerals).

## Economic relevance
**Cement** set regulator and a source of sulfuric acid; hydrates to gypsum plaster.

## Identification notes
- Harder than gypsum, no water content, associated with evaporites; hydrates (swells) on
> wetting — the reverse of gypsum dehydrating to anhydrite.
