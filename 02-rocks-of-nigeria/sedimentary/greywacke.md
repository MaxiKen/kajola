# Greywacke

> **Family:** Sedimentary — clastic · **Grain size:** sand (poorly sorted)
> **Key feature:** Dark, poorly sorted, matrix-rich sandstone · **Quick ID:** Dark, gritty, poorly sorted, feels abrasive — the "dirty" end of the sandstone spectrum.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Dark grey, green-grey, black |
| Texture | Poorly sorted, gritty, matrix-rich |
| Sorting | **Poor** (mixed grain sizes) |
| Key clue | Dark + gritty + abundant fine matrix |
| Setting | Turbidite (deep-water) sequences |
| Hardness | Harder/darker than clean sandstone |

## Composition
A dark, **poorly sorted sandstone** rich in **rock fragments and a clay matrix**, with quartz and feldspar — typical of **turbidite** (deep-water) sequences. The abundant fine matrix (clay) between the sand grains is what makes it "dirty" and dark.

## Origin & classification
Greywacke is a **clastic sedimentary rock** — an **immature, poorly sorted sandstone** deposited rapidly (typically by **turbidity currents** in deep water) so that grains were not sorted or cleaned. On the QFL diagram it is a **litharenite/wacke** — rich in rock fragments and matrix, unlike mature quartz arenite.

## Texture & sedimentary structures
Poorly sorted, gritty, and matrix-rich; darker and harder than clean sandstone. Often shows **graded bedding** (coarse at base, fine at top of each bed) — the classic **Bouma sequence** of turbidites. Individual grains are hard to see because of the abundant dark matrix.

## Diagnostic field features
- Dark, gritty, poorly sorted; feels abrasive.
- Darker and more "dirty" (matrix-rich) than a clean quartz sandstone.
- Hard and compact.
- May show graded bedding.

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Colour:** dark grey/black/green-grey (matrix-rich).
2. **Sorting:** poorly sorted — grains of many sizes mixed.
3. **Feel:** gritty/abrasive but with a fine matrix ("dirty").
4. **Vs clean sandstone:** greywacke is darker and matrix-rich; quartz sandstone is pale and clean.
5. **Vs shale:** greywacke is gritty (sand-sized grains); shale is smooth and very fine.

## Depositional setting & formation
Greywacke forms in **deep-water** settings where **turbidity currents** (dense, sediment-laden underwater flows) dump poorly sorted sediment rapidly. These turbidite sequences build up thick, graded beds. In Nigeria, greywacke occurs in **metasedimentary (metagreywacke)** terrains of the Basement.

## Host states / localities
Basement **metasedimentary** terrains (as **metagreywacke**) and some sedimentary sequences — **Niger, Kwara, Kogi, Anambra, Benue**, etc. (see [Metasediments](../metamorphic/metasediments.md)).

## Associated minerals & rocks
Shale and metasediments; with metamorphism becomes **metagreywacke/quartz schist**. Associated rocks: shale, turbidite sequences, metagreywacke.

## Economic relevance
- **Aggregate** and building stone.
- **Turbidite aquifers** — greywacke sequences can host groundwater.
- Can host mineralization (e.g., **gold** in deformed/metamorphosed equivalents).
- Indicator of **deep-water (turbidite)** deposition.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Clean sandstone** | Greywacke is dark, poorly sorted, matrix-rich; quartz sandstone is pale and clean. |
| **Metagreywacke** | Metagreywacke is metamorphic (foliated, recrystallised); greywacke is sedimentary. |
| **Shale** | Greywacke is gritty (sand-sized); shale is smooth and very fine. |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Dark + gritty + poorly sorted?
- [ ] Abundant fine matrix ("dirty")?
- [ ] Feels abrasive?
- [ ] Graded bedding (turbidite)?
- [ ] Deep-water / metasedimentary setting?

## Field tips
- Dark, poorly sorted, gritty sandstone with abundant matrix — the "dirty" end of the sandstone spectrum (see [Sandstone, Grit & Arkose](sandstone.md)).
- Look for **graded bedding** — a turbidite clue.
- Greywacke weathers to a dark, clay-rich soil.
- In the Basement it is often metamorphosed to **metagreywacke**.

## Facts
- Greywacke is a **poorly sorted, matrix-rich sandstone** — the "dirty" end of the sandstone family.
- It is typically deposited by **turbidity currents** in deep water.
- **Graded bedding** (coarse base, fine top) is its signature sedimentary structure.
- With metamorphism it becomes **metagreywacke**, common in the Basement.
