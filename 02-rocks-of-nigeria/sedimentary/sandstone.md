# Sandstone, Grit & Arkose

> **Family:** Sedimentary — clastic · **Grain size:** sand (0.06–2 mm)

## Composition
**Quartz** and **feldspar** grains cemented by **silica, calcite, or iron oxide**.
- **Sandstone:** quartz-dominated.
- **Arkose:** feldspar-rich (from rapidly weathered granite).
- **Grit / conglomeratic sandstone:** coarse, pebbly.

## Texture
**Clastic and granular** — visible sand grains; often **porous and permeable**;
typically **bedded (layered)**. Sorting and rounding vary with transport distance.

## Diagnostic field features
- **Gritty** — grains visible and feel like sandpaper.
- Forms in **beds/layers**; often tan, red, brown, or white (iron staining).
- Quartz grains are **hard (won't scratch)**.
- **Calcite-cemented** sandstone may faintly **fizz** with acid.

## Host states / localities
The dominant rock of the basins — **Benue Trough** (Bima Sandstone),
**Niger Delta** (Benin Formation — the freshwater aquifer), **Chad**, **Sokoto**:
**Enugu, Benue, Kogi, Cross River, Plateau, Borno, Sokoto, Delta, Anambra**, and more.

## Associated minerals
Quartz, feldspar, iron oxides; fossils uncommon; may carry **uranium** in some
sandstones; hosts **groundwater** and **oil/gas**.

## Economic relevance
- **Building/glass sand** and **construction aggregate**.
- **Aquifer** — a key groundwater source (Benin Formation).
- **Reservoir rock** for petroleum in the Niger Delta.
- **Dimension/facing stone.**

## Identification notes
- **Gritty, granular, bedded** = sandstone.
- **vs. limestone:** sandstone doesn't (usually) fizz; limestone does.
- **vs. granite:** sandstone grains are loose/cemented and layered; granite is
  interlocking and massive.
