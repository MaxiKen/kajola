# Calcrete & Silcrete (Duricrusts)

> **Family:** Sedimentary / duricrust · **Origin:** near-surface cementation of soil/regolith
> **Varieties:** Calcrete (caliche, CaCO₃ cement) · Silcrete (silica cement)
> **Quick ID:** Hard, resistant surface crust; **calcrete fizzes** (carbonate), **silcrete** is extremely hard (silica).

## At a glance

| Property | Calcrete | Silcrete |
|---|---|---|
| Cement | **Calcium carbonate** (CaCO₃) | **Silica** (SiO₂) |
| Acid test | **Fizzes** with dilute acid | No fizz |
| Hardness | Moderate | Very hard (silica) |
| Climate | Arid/semi-arid | Various (often arid) |
| Setting | Northern Sahelian regions | Parts of the north |

## Composition
Near-surface **duricrusts** — hard caps formed by cementation of soil/regolith:
- **Calcrete (caliche)** — cemented by **calcium carbonate**.
- **Silcrete** — cemented by **silica**.

They are **secondary** crusts — minerals precipitated within the weathered profile, cementing loose material into a hard layer.

## Origin & classification
Duricrusts form by **near-surface cementation** — dissolved minerals (calcium carbonate or silica) are carried down by water and precipitate within the soil/regolith, gluing it into a hard cap. **Calcrete** signals **arid/semi-arid** climates (where carbonate accumulates); **silcrete** forms where silica is mobilised and reprecipitated. They are the arid-climate equivalents of laterite.

## Texture & sedimentary structures
Hard, massive, nodular, or laminated surface layers developed within the weathered profile. Calcrete is often **nodular** with carbonate veins; silcrete is massive and very hard. Both cap softer, uncemented material below.

## Diagnostic field features
- Hard, resistant crust capping softer material below.
- **Calcrete fizzes** in dilute acid (calcite cement); **silcrete** is extremely hard (silica cement).
- Forms **caprock** that protects softer material from erosion.
- Nodular, honeycombed, or laminated textures.

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Acid:** calcrete **fizzes**; silcrete does **not**.
2. **Hardness:** silcrete is extremely hard (scratches steel); calcrete is moderate.
3. **Form:** hard crust capping softer material below.
4. **Setting:** arid/semi-arid (calcrete) regions.
5. **Vs laterite:** laterite is iron-rich (reddish, no fizz); calcrete is carbonate (fizzes).

## Depositional setting & formation
Duricrusts form in the **near-surface zone** where downward-percolating water dissolves and reprecipitates carbonate or silica, cementing the soil. Calcrete is typical of **arid/semi-arid** climates (low leaching lets carbonate accumulate); it is a **paleoclimate indicator** — its presence records past drier conditions.

## Host states / localities
Common in the **Sahelian/northern** regions — **Borno, Yobe, Sokoto, Katsina, Jigawa, Kebbi** (calcrete especially); silcrete in parts of the north (see [Laterite & Ferricrete](laterite-ferricrete.md)). Develops in the seasonally dry north.

## Associated minerals & rocks
Sand, laterite, and near-surface groundwater; paleoclimate indicators. Associated rocks: sandstone, laterite, alluvium.

## Economic relevance
- Calcrete for **lime and road metal**.
- Duricrusts strongly affect **groundwater, foundations, and construction** (hard caps impede drilling/roots).
- Indicators of past **arid climates** (paleoclimate reconstruction).
- Silcrete was used as a **stone-tool material** archaeologically.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Laterite** | Laterite is iron-rich (reddish, no fizz); calcrete is carbonate (fizzes). |
| **Sandstone** | Sandstone is a primary bedded rock; duricrusts are near-surface cemented crusts. |
| **Limestone** | Limestone is a primary bedded carbonate; calcrete is a nodular near-surface crust. |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Hard crust capping softer material?
- [ ] Fizzes (calcrete) or very hard/no fizz (silcrete)?
- [ ] Nodular/laminated/honeycombed?
- [ ] Arid/northern setting?
- [ ] Near-surface (duricrust, not bedrock)?

## Field tips
- Hard surface crust; test with acid (**calcrete fizzes, silcrete doesn't**).
- Duricrusts record **past arid climates** — a paleoclimate clue.
- They strongly affect **construction and groundwater** (hard caps impede drilling).
- Distinguish calcrete (fizzes) from silcrete (very hard) and laterite (reddish).

## Facts
- **Calcrete** (caliche) is a carbonate crust of arid regions; **silcrete** is its silica equivalent.
- Duricrusts are **paleoclimate indicators** — calcrete records past aridity.
- They form hard **caprock** that shapes the landscape and impedes drilling.
- Silcrete was prized by early humans for **stone tools**.
