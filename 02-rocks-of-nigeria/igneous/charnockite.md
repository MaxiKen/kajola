# Charnockite

> **Family:** Igneous/metamorphic — granulite-facies · **Composition:** Felsic (quartz + hypersthene + feldspar)

## Composition
**Quartz, alkali feldspar, plagioclase**, and the diagnostic **hypersthene**
(an orthopyroxene, giving a greenish-bronze sheen), ± garnet. Essentially a
**quartz–hypersthene granite** formed under **high temperature and pressure**
(granulite-facies) conditions.

## Texture
**Granular to porphyritic**; often shows **granulitic** texture (equant, interlocking
grains). May be gneissic (banded) in places.

## Diagnostic field features
- Granite-like but contains **greenish/bronzy hypersthene** (a pyroxene) — the giveaway.
- Commonly **greenish-grey to dark**; sometimes marketed as brown/black "granite."
- Hard, massive; weathers to rounded boulders.

## Host states / localities
Occurs as **charnockite massifs** in the **northern and north-central Basement** —
reported in **Kaduna, Plateau, Bauchi, Katsina**, and nearby areas. (Named after
Job Charnock's tombstone in Calcutta, India.)

## Associated minerals
Apatite, magnetite, garnet; associated granulite-facies minerals.

## Economic relevance
- **Dimension/ornamental stone** (sold as granite) — a growing quarrying sector.
- **Aggregate**.
- Granulite terranes can be targets for **rare metals and graphite**.

## Identification notes
- **Greenish hypersthene** in a granite-like rock = charnockite.
- Distinguish from ordinary granite by the **pyroxene** content.
- High-grade (granulite) look: sugary, equigranular.
