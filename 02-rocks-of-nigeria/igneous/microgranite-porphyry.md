# Microgranite & Porphyry

> **Family:** Igneous — hypabyssal · **Composition:** Felsic (microgranite) / variable (porphyry texture)

## Composition
- **Microgranite:** the **fine-grained equivalent of granite** (same felsic minerals,
  but cooled quickly in shallow dykes/sills).
- **Porphyry:** defined by **texture, not composition** — large crystals
  (**phenocrysts**) set in a fine-grained groundmass. Porphyries can be felsic,
  intermediate, or mafic.

## Texture
**Fine-grained (aphanitic)** with visible **phenocrysts** — this is the
**porphyritic texture**: big early-formed crystals floating in a fine matrix.

## Diagnostic field features
- Look closely (hand lens): **large crystals in a fine matrix**.
- Microgranite: pale, fine-grained, granite composition.
- Porphyry: prominent phenocrysts (often feldspar) in a dense groundmass.

## Host states / localities
Margins and dykes of the **Younger Granite** complexes and Basement intrusions:
**Plateau, Bauchi, Kaduna, Nasarawa**, and elsewhere as dykes.

## Associated minerals
Generally minor; porphyritic textures can be associated with **porphyry-style
copper–gold–molybdenum** systems elsewhere in the world (not a major Nigerian
deposit type, but worth recognizing the texture).

## Economic relevance
- **Aggregate** and building stone.
- Recognizing **porphyritic texture** is key to spotting potential porphyry-style
  mineralization when combined with alteration/geochemistry.

## Identification notes
- **Porphyritic texture** = phenocrysts in a fine matrix. (See the porphyritic basalt
  image in [Basalt](basalt.md) for a clear example.)
- Microgranite vs. rhyolite: both fine-grained and felsic; microgranite is intrusive
  (hypabyssal), rhyolite is extrusive (volcanic) — often hard to tell in hand specimen.
