# Volcanic Tuff, Ignimbrite & Agglomerate

## Composition
**Volcaniclastic** rocks — fragmented volcanic material. **Tuff** = consolidated volcanic
ash; **ignimbrite** = a hot, **welded** ash-flow (pyroclastic-flow) deposit; **agglomerate**
= coarse volcanic bombs/blocks.

## Texture
Fine-grained and fragmental; ignimbrites show a **welded, flame-like "eutaxitic" texture**
with flattened pumice fragments (**fiammé**); aglomerates are coarse and angular/rounded.

## Diagnostic field features
- Light-coloured, fine, fragmental; looks like a solidified ash.
- Ignimbrite: flattened/streaked pumice fragments; agglomerate: obvious volcanic bombs.
- Distinguish from lava flows by the **fragmental** (clastic) texture.

## Host states / localities
**Plateau (Jos), Bauchi, Nasarawa, Kaduna** — the volcanic phase of the Younger Granite
ring complexes (e.g., rhyolitic–trachytic tuffs and ignimbrites).

## Associated minerals
Rhyolite, trachyte, phonolite, granite; crystals of quartz, feldspar, sanidine.

## Economic relevance
Dimension/building stone, aggregate; volcaniclastic horizons can localize mineralization.

## Identification notes
- Look for fragmental texture, fiammé, and association with acid/intermediate volcanics.
- Generally lighter and softer than the associated lavas.
