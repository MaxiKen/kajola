# Syenite & Nepheline Syenite

> **Family:** Igneous — intrusive (plutonic) · **Composition:** Intermediate–felsic, alkali-rich, quartz-poor
> **Extrusive equivalents:** Syenite → trachyte; Nepheline syenite → phonolite · **Mean density:** 2.6–2.8 g/cm³ · **Silica:** ~50–65 wt% (low)
> **Quick ID:** Looks like granite but has **little or no quartz**; dominated by pale feldspar; nepheline syenite has greasy, acid-gelatinising feldspathoid.

## At a glance

| Property | Syenite | Nepheline syenite |
|---|---|---|
| Quartz | Little or none | **None** (silica-undersaturated) |
| Key minerals | Alkali feldspar + hornblende/biotite | Alkali feldspar + **nepheline** (feldspathoid) |
| Colour | Grey / pink | Grey, often with greasy lustre |
| Texture | Coarse (phaneritic) | Coarse (phaneritic) |
| Hardness | ~6 | ~5.5–6 |
| Specific gravity | ~2.6–2.7 | ~2.6–2.8 |
| Acid test | No fizz | **Nepheline gelatinises in acid** |

## Composition
- **Syenite:** mostly **alkali feldspar** (orthoclase/microcline) with **hornblende and/or biotite**, and **little or no quartz** — this lack of quartz is the defining feature separating it from granite.
- **Nepheline syenite:** alkali feldspar + **nepheline** (a **feldspathoid** mineral that forms when there is too little silica to make quartz) plus alkali amphibole/pyroxene (**aegirine, riebeckite**). It is **silica-undersaturated**.

Chemically **alkali-rich, silica-poor**: high Na₂O+K₂O, moderate SiO₂ (syenite ≈ 55–65%; nepheline syenite ≈ 50–60%), characteristic of an **alkaline magma series**.

## Geochemistry
- **Major oxides:** SiO₂ 50–65 wt%, Al₂O₃ 15–20%, (Na₂O+K₂O) 8–14% (high), CaO low (1–4%), FeO+MgO 2–6%.
- **Trace elements:** very high **Zr, Nb, REE, Li, Be, F, Cl**; high-field-strength-element (HFSE) enrichment is the fingerprint of alkaline magmas.
- **Nature in Nigeria:** the syenites/nepheline syenites of the **Younger Granite ring complexes** are **peralkaline to metaluminous alkaline** rocks, commonly enriched in REE, Zr, and Nb — a fertile signature for rare-metal exploration.

## Texture
**Coarse-grained (phaneritic)**; sometimes **trachytic** (aligned feldspar laths from flow). Nepheline syenite may be coarse and equigranular, occasionally showing large feldspathoid crystals.

## Diagnostic field features
- Looks **granite-like but lacks visible quartz** — a critical check.
- Dominated by **pale feldspar** with less dark mineral than diorite.
- **Nepheline syenite** is typically **grey**, and **nepheline shows a greasy lustre** and, when powdered and treated with acid, **gelatinises** (a distinctive test).
- May host brightly coloured alkaline minerals (blue **sodalite**, red/pink **cancrinite**, green **aegirine**).

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Hunt for quartz:** if you cannot find glassy quartz grains in a feldspar-rich coarse rock, think syenite.
2. **Acid on suspect pale mineral:** powder a pale grain and add dilute HCl — if it **gelatinises** (forms a jelly), it is **nepheline** → nepheline syenite.
3. **Hand lens:** confirm feldspar dominance and the dark mineral (hornblende/aegirine).
4. **Context:** alkaline syenites sit in zoned ring complexes — note neighbours.

## Genesis & tectonic setting
Syenite and nepheline syenite crystallise from **alkaline, silica-poor magma** in anorogenic (within-plate) settings. In Nigeria they are integral to the **Younger Granite ring complexes** of the Jos Plateau — alkaline complexes that commonly build a zoned sequence (granite → syenite → nepheline syenite → carbonatite core). Rare in the older, calc-alkaline Basement.

## Host states / localities
Chiefly in the **Younger Granite ring complexes** of the **Jos Plateau** and environs: **Plateau, Bauchi, Kaduna, Nasarawa**. These alkaline complexes commonly build a zoned sequence (syenite → nepheline syenite → carbonatite). Rare in the older Basement, which is mostly calc-alkaline granite–granodiorite.

## Associated minerals & rocks
- **Nepheline syenites** may host **zircon, aegirine, riebeckite, sodalite, cancrinite, eudialyte, rare-earth minerals**.
- **Syenites** may carry **apatite, magnetite, titanite (sphene)**.
- Associated rocks: granite, trachyte/phonolite, and carbonatite in alkaline ring complexes.

## Economic relevance
- **Nepheline syenite** is a valuable **industrial mineral** — a **substitute for feldspar** in **glass, ceramics, and alumina** production (especially important where bauxite is scarce). Some African nepheline syenites are mined for exactly this.
- Potential host for **rare-earth elements (REE)** and **zirconium (zircon)**.
- **Apatite** in syenites can be a source of **phosphate**.
- **Dimension stone / aggregate**.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Granite** | Granite has abundant **quartz**; syenite has little/none. |
| **Diorite** | Diorite is darker (more hornblende) and lacks the feldspar dominance. |
| **Trachyte / phonolite** | Same composition but fine-grained **extrusive** rocks. |
| **Marble / calc-silicate** | Marble reacts strongly with acid (fizzes) all over. |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Feldspar-dominated coarse rock?
- [ ] Little/no quartz found?
- [ ] No acid fizz on the bulk rock?
- [ ] Pale mineral gelatinises in acid (→ nepheline)?
- [ ] Colourful alkaline minerals (sodalite/aegirine)?
- [ ] Part of a zoned alkaline complex?

## Field tips
- **Hunt for quartz**: if you cannot find glassy quartz grains in a feldspar-rich coarse rock, think syenite.
- Test suspicious pale minerals with **dilute acid**: gelatinising = **nepheline**.
- Alkaline complexes are **zoned** — map the sequence from syenite outward to carbonatite; this zonation is a mineralisation guide.
- Look for colourful accessory minerals (sodalite, aegirine) as clues to the alkaline, feldspathoid-bearing character.

## Facts
- **Syenite** is named after **Syene** (modern Aswan), Egypt — though that ancient "syenite" is actually more like granite.
- **Nepheline syenite** is the rock of many of the world's **rare-earth and zirconium** deposits.
- In alkaline ring complexes, syenite and nepheline syenite typically sit **outside** granite and **inside** the carbonatite core — a textbook zoning pattern seen in the Jos-type complexes.
- **Nepheline** is essentially a **"silica-poor feldspar"** — it forms instead of feldspar+quartz when silica runs short.
