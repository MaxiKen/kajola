# Granite (Older Granites & granite *sensu lato*)

> **Family:** Igneous — intrusive (plutonic) · **Composition:** Felsic

## Composition
Chiefly **quartz**, **alkali feldspar (orthoclase)**, **plagioclase feldspar**, and
**mica** (biotite ± muscovite), with minor hornblende. Chemically **silica-rich**.

## Texture
**Coarse-grained (phaneritic)** — interlocking crystals easily visible to the naked
eye, formed by **slow cooling** of magma underground. Usually equigranular, sometimes
porphyritic.

## Diagnostic field features
- "Salt-and-pepper" mix of glassy grey **quartz**, pink/white **feldspar**, and
  black **mica**.
- **No layering or alignment** (that would make it gneiss).
- **Hard** (scratches glass); feels heavy and dense; no reaction to acid.
- Interlocking (not loose) grains.

## Host states / localities
The most abundant rock of the **Basement Complex** and the **Younger Granites**.
Quarried nationwide: **FCT (Abuja), Ogun (Abeokuta), Oyo, Ondo, Ekiti, Osun, Kwara,
Niger, Plateau, Kaduna, Kano, Bauchi**, and more.

## Associated minerals
In nearby **pegmatites**: **cassiterite, columbite–tantalite, wolframite, mica,
feldspar, quartz**, and gemstones. Granite itself yields **dimension stone** and
**aggregate**.

## Economic relevance
- **Dimension/ornamental stone** — one of Nigeria's biggest quarrying industries
  (tiles, slabs, monuments).
- **Construction aggregate**.
- Host rock for **tin/columbite/tungsten** in associated pegmatites (Jos Plateau).

## Identification notes
- **vs. gneiss:** granite has **no** banding/foliation; gneiss does.
- **vs. syenite:** syenite has little/no quartz.
- **vs. granodiorite/diorite:** those have more dark (mafic) minerals and less quartz.
- **vs. sandstone:** sandstone is grainy/loose and layered; granite is interlocking and massive.
