# Granodiorite & Diorite

> **Family:** Igneous — intrusive (plutonic) · **Composition:** Granodiorite = intermediate; Diorite = intermediate-mafic
> **Extrusive equivalents:** Granodiorite → dacite; Diorite → andesite · **Mean density:** 2.7–2.9 g/cm³ · **Silica:** ~55–70 wt% SiO₂
> **Quick ID:** Granite-like but with **more dark mineral and less quartz**; diorite = black-and-white "salt-and-pepper," little/no quartz.

## At a glance

| Property | Granodiorite | Diorite |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Grey to pinkish | Grey / black-and-white |
| Quartz | Moderate (5–20%) | Little to none (<5%) |
| Key minerals | Quartz, plagioclase > alkali feldspar, biotite, hornblende | Plagioclase + hornblende/biotite |
| Texture | Coarse (phaneritic) | Coarse (phaneritic) |
| Hardness | ~6–7 | ~6–7 |
| Specific gravity | ~2.7–2.8 | ~2.8–2.9 |
| Setting | Basement plutons; Younger Granite margins | Basement intrusions, pluton margins |

## Composition
- **Granodiorite:** quartz + **plagioclase feldspar exceeding alkali feldspar**, plus biotite and hornblende — chemically and mineralogically **between granite and diorite**. More dark (mafic) mineral than granite, more quartz than diorite.
- **Diorite:** **plagioclase feldspar + hornblende and/or biotite**, with **little or no quartz**. The **hornblende (amphibole)** — not pyroxene — is the key dark mineral, which is what separates it from gabbro.

On the QAPF diagram, diorite has quartz <5% and plagioclase >90% of the feldspars; granodiorite has quartz 20–60% with plagioclase 65–90% of feldspars. Chemically **intermediate**: SiO₂ ≈ 55–65% (diorite) to 65–70% (granodiorite).

## Geochemistry
- **Major oxides:** SiO₂ 55–70 wt%, Al₂O₃ 15–18%, CaO 3–7%, (Na₂O+K₂O) 4–7%, FeO+MgO 4–8% (higher than granite).
- **Trace elements:** moderate **Sr, Ba, V, Ni, Cr** (mafic inheritance); **calc-alkaline** affinity; LILE-enriched (subduction-related signature in the Older Granitoids).
- **Nature in Nigeria:** the **Older Granitoids** (tonalite–trondhjemite–granodiorite, "TTG") and calc-alkaline granodiorite–diorite are abundant in the western and northern Basement — products of the Pan-African magmatic arc.

## Texture
**Coarse-grained (phaneritic)**, interlocking crystals from slow underground cooling. Diorite is often **hypidiomorphic** (subhedral plagioclase laths) with a strikingly speckled look; granodiorite may be **porphyritic** near intrusion margins.

## Diagnostic field features
- **Diorite:** a bold black-and-white "salt-and-pepper" of black **hornblende** and white **plagioclase**, with **little/no quartz**.
- **Granodiorite:** looks granite-like but with **more dark mineral** and **less obvious quartz and pink feldspar**.
- **Hard, massive** (no layering), non-magnetic to weakly magnetic.
- Hornblende appears as **elongated black/green grains** with two cleavage directions (~120°), distinct from stubby pyroxene.

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Estimate quartz:** scan for glassy grey grains — diorite shows almost none; granodiorite shows a moderate amount.
2. **Hand lens the dark mineral:** hornblende = elongated, two cleavages ~120°, greenish-black; pyroxene = stubby, cleavages ~90°. Diorite should show hornblende, not pyroxene.
3. **Acid:** no fizz (unless it borders carbonate/skarn).
4. **Hardness:** 6–7; a knife scratches but glass is also scratched.
5. **Context:** near limestone, look for garnet-rich **skarn** at the contact.

## Genesis & tectonic setting
Granodiorite and diorite crystallise from **intermediate-composition magma** (andesitic) cooling at depth. In Nigeria they form much of the **Older Granitoid** suite intruded during the **Pan-African orogeny** (magmatic-arc and syn-collisional plutons, ~600–750 Ma), and appear as mafic-rich margins of the Younger Granite complexes.

## Host states / localities
Common in the **Basement Complex** and as **marginal / mafic phases of the Younger Granite** ring complexes: **Plateau, Kaduna, Bauchi, Oyo, Ondo, Niger, Kwara**, and across the shield as calc-alkaline plutons. The **Older Granitoids** (granodiorite–tonalite) are abundant in the western and northern Basement.

## Associated minerals & rocks
- **Magnetite, sphene (titanite), apatite, zircon**; minor **sulfides**.
- Where diorite intrudes limestone: **skarn minerals** — **garnet, pyroxene, scheelite (tungsten), sulfides**.
- Associated rocks: granite, gabbro, migmatite, and marble/carbonate in the aureole.

## Economic relevance
- **Aggregate** and **dimension / building stone**.
- **Skarn (contact) zones** with limestone can carry **iron, tungsten (scheelite), copper, and gold**.
- Local source of **titanium** (titanite) and **magnetite**.
- Recognised as host rock for small **gold** and **base-metal** showings in altered zones.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Granite** | Granite has more quartz (glassy grey) and more pink alkali feldspar; granodiorite/diorite have more dark mineral and less quartz. |
| **Gabbro** | Gabbro is darker and dominated by **pyroxene** (blocky, 2 cleavages ~90°) rather than **hornblende** (elongated, ~120°). |
| **Andesite / dacite** | Same composition, but fine-grained **extrusive** rocks. |
| **Charnockite** | Contains **hypersthene** (orthopyroxene) and is granulite-facies. |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Dark mineral present but rock still pale overall?
- [ ] Quartz low (diorite) or moderate (granodiorite)?
- [ ] Dark mineral = **hornblende** (elongated), not pyroxene?
- [ ] No banding (rules out gneiss)?
- [ ] No acid fizz?
- [ ] Near limestone? Look for skarn.

## Field tips
- Distinguish **hornblende vs pyroxene** with a hand lens: hornblende elongated with cleavages at ~120°; pyroxene stubby with cleavages near 90°.
- Estimate **quartz content** by eye — diorite should show almost none.
- Near carbonate rocks, look for **garnet-rich skarn** at contacts (a mineralisation clue).
- Note the salt-and-pepper appearance; it weathers to a sandy, dark-speckled soil.

## Facts
- Diorite's black-and-white speckle made it a favourite for **ornamental stone and ancient sculpture** (the Code of Hammurabi is carved from diorite).
- In Nigeria much of the "salt-and-pepper" stone used in construction is **diorite or granodiorite** marketed as granite.
- **Scheelite** (a tungsten ore) is found in skarns where diorite meets carbonate — a classic **contact metasomatic** deposit type.
- The **Older Granitoids** (diorite–granodiorite–tonalite) are among the **oldest rocks** in the Nigerian Basement.
