# Talc / Chlorite Schist (Soapstone)

> **Family:** Metamorphic (low grade) · **Protolith:** ultramafic/mafic rock (altered)
> **Key feature:** Very soft, soapy, green — dominated by talc and/or chlorite · **Quick ID:** Soapy feel + very soft (fingernail-scratchable) + green = soapstone.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Green (chlorite) to pale green/white (talc) |
| Feel | **Soapy / greasy** |
| Hardness | **Very soft** — talc = 1 (fingernail-scratchable) |
| Texture | Schistose to massive, soft |
| Key minerals | Talc and/or chlorite; serpentine, magnesite, magnetite |
| Protolith | Ultramafic / mafic rock (altered) |

## Composition
Dominated by soft, platy minerals — **talc** and/or **chlorite** — with serpentine, magnesite, and magnetite. Formed by **hydration/alteration of ultramafic rocks** (peridotite/pyroxenite). Talc-rich varieties are called **soapstone** or **steatite**.

## Protolith & metamorphic grade
Talc/chlorite schist forms by **low-grade alteration of ultramafic or mafic rock** — olivine/pyroxene react with water and CO₂ to form soft **talc, chlorite, and serpentine**. It represents the **softest** end of the metamorphic/altered-rock spectrum, grading into serpentinite (more serpentine) and greenschist (more chlorite from basalt).

## Texture & foliation
**Schistose to massive**, soft, often greasy-feeling; green (chlorite) to pale green/white (talc). Talc gives a pronounced soapy feel; the rock can often be **carved** with a knife.

## Diagnostic field features
- **Very soft** — talc has hardness **1** (scratched by a fingernail).
- **Soapy/greasy feel** — hence "soapstone."
- **Green** colour (chlorite/serpentine).
- Sometimes contains **asbestos (chrysotile)** fibres and **magnesite**.

## Identification tests — step by step
1. **Fingernail test:** talc-rich soapstone is so soft a **fingernail** scratches it.
2. **Feel:** distinctly soapy/greasy.
3. **Colour:** green to pale green/white.
4. **Hand lens:** soft platy talc/chlorite; serpentine; magnesite.
5. **Vs serpentinite:** talc schist is dominated by talc (softer); serpentinite by serpentine.

## Genesis & setting
Talc/chlorite schist forms by **hydration and alteration of ultramafic/mafic rock** — hot, water-rich fluids convert hard olivine/pyroxene into soft talc, chlorite, and serpentine. It occurs in altered ultramafic zones, often alongside serpentinite.

## Host states / localities
Altered ultramafic zones in the **Basement Complex**: reported in parts of **Kwara, Oyo, Kaduna**, and some schist belts. Generally minor occurrences.

## Associated minerals & rocks
Talc, chlorite, serpentine, magnesite; sometimes **asbestos (chrysotile)**, **nickel, chromite** (from the parent ultramafic). Associated rocks: serpentinite, peridotite/pyroxenite, greenschist.

## Economic relevance
- **Talc** — cosmetics, ceramics, paper, paint, plastics.
- **Soapstone** — carving, countertops, stove liners, sculptures.
- **Asbestos** (handle with care) and **magnesia/magnesium** compounds.
- **Nickel/chromite** potential from the parent ultramafic.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Serpentinite** | Serpentinite is dominated by serpentine (moderately soft); talc schist is dominated by talc (hardness 1, soapier). |
| **Greenschist** | Greenschist is chlorite/epidote from basalt (moderately soft); talc schist is much softer (soapy). |
| **Chlorite schist (greenstone) | Talc schist is distinguished by its **extreme softness** (talc, hardness 1). |

## Field checklist
- [ ] Soapy/greasy feel?
- [ ] Very soft (fingernail-scratchable)?
- [ ] Green (chlorite) to pale (talc)?
- [ ] From an ultramafic/mafic body?
- [ ] Asbestos fibres / magnesite present?

## Field tips
- **Soapy feel + very soft + green** = talc/chlorite schist (soapstone).
- Distinguished from chlorite-rich **greenstone** by extreme softness (talc).
- **Handle asbestos-bearing varieties with care** — avoid generating dust.
- Soapstone bodies are a source of **talc** (industrial) and are easily recognised by their soapy feel.

## Facts
- **Talc is the softest mineral** on Earth (hardness 1) — a fingernail scratches it.
- **Soapstone** (talc-rich) has been carved into bowls, stoves, and sculptures for millennia.
- Talc/chlorite schist forms by **alteration of ultramafic rock** (with serpentinite).
- Its soapy feel comes from the platy talc grains sliding over each other.
