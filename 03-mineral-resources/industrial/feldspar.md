# Feldspar

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic · **Minerals:** K-feldspar (orthoclase) & plagioclase

## What it is
The **feldspar group** — the most abundant minerals in Earth's crust. Main types:
**orthoclase/microcline** (K-feldspar) and **plagioclase** (Na–Ca feldspar). A major
constituent of **granites and pegmatites**.

## Properties & how to recognize
- **Hardness 6** (scratches glass).
- **Two cleavages at ~90°** (right angles) — a diagnostic feature.
- White, pink, or grey; vitreous; often with a **porcelain-like** luster.
- Weathers to **clay (kaolin)** — a useful field clue.

## Where in Nigeria (states)
Recovered from **granites and pegmatites** across the Basement and Younger Granites:
**Plateau, Kaduna, Oyo, Kwara, Kogi, Nasarawa, Ondo**, and elsewhere.

## Uses
- **GLASS** and **CERAMICS** (glazes, bodies) — the main uses.
- **Filler** in paint, rubber, plastics; **abrasives**.

## Exploration / notes
- Separate feldspar from **quartz** (no cleavage) by its **two right-angle cleavages**.
- Common as a **by-product** of granite/pegmatite quarrying.
- Weathered (kaolinized) feldspar marks good **kaolin** potential.
