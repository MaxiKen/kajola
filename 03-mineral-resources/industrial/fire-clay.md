# Fire Clay (Refractory Clay)

## Chemistry & mineralogy
A **refractory clay** — hydrous aluminium silicates (kaolinite-group minerals) with
**high alumina and low fluxing impurities** (iron, alkalis), giving it a high melting
point.

## Crystal habit
Massive, earthy, plastic when wet, and fissile (breaks into plates).

## Physical properties & how to test
- **Hardness:** soft and earthy when dry.
- **Colour:** white, grey, or buff.
- **Signature:** **high fusion/refractoriness** (resists high heat); plastic when wet,
  hard when fired.

## Genesis
Residual and hydrothermal alteration, and as **underclays ("seat earths") beneath coal
seams** in the Benue Trough and other sedimentary basins.

## States / localities
**Kogi, Enugu, Benue** — associated with the coal measures and their underclays.

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** **refractories** — firebricks, crucibles, furnace/kiln linings, kiln
  furniture; high-duty ceramics.
- **Market:** refractory-grade clay commands a premium for **low iron and high alumina**.

## Exploration notes
- Target **underclays of coal measures** (see [Coal](../energy/coal.md)).
- Assay **refractoriness (PCE/orion), Al₂O₃**, and low Fe/alkali content.
- Grades into [Clay](clay.md) and [Kaolin](kaolin.md) depending on purity.
