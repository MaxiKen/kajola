# Fire Clay (Refractory Clay)

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic · **Material:** kaolinite-group clay (high Al₂O₃, low fluxes)
> **Quick ID:** White/grey/buff, soft and earthy; **high refractoriness** (resists high heat); plastic when wet, hard when fired.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | White, grey, or buff |
| Texture | Soft and earthy when dry; plastic when wet |
| Key test | **High refractoriness** (resists high heat) |
| Chemistry | High alumina, low fluxing impurities (Fe, alkalis) |
| Genesis | Residual/hydrothermal; underclays beneath coal |

## Chemistry & mineralogy
A **refractory clay** — hydrous aluminium silicates (kaolinite-group minerals) with **high alumina and low fluxing impurities** (iron, alkalis), giving it a high melting point. Low iron = whiter and more refractory.

## Crystal habit
Massive, earthy, plastic when wet, and fissile (breaks into plates). Identified by its bulk refractoriness and clay behaviour, not crystals.

## Physical properties
- **Hardness:** soft and earthy when dry.
- **Colour:** white, grey, or buff.
- **Signature:** **high fusion/refractoriness** (resists high heat); plastic when wet, hard when fired.

## How to identify in the field
1. **Refractoriness:** resists very high heat (used in firebricks).
2. **Plasticity:** plastic when wet, hard when fired.
3. **Colour:** white, grey, or buff (low iron).
4. **Texture:** soft, earthy, fissile.
5. **Context:** underclays beneath coal seams; residual alteration.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Common clay** | Common clay has more fluxes (lower melting); fire clay is high-refractory. |
| **Kaolin** | Kaolin is white and fine but not necessarily high-refractory; fire clay grades for heat resistance. |
| **Shale** | Shale is hard and laminated; fire clay is soft and earthy. |

## Genesis
Residual and hydrothermal alteration, and as **underclays ("seat earths") beneath coal seams** in the Benue Trough and other sedimentary basins. Fire clays often occur directly under coal seams.

## States / localities
**Kogi, Enugu, Benue** — associated with the coal measures and their underclays.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Kaolinite, illite, quartz, siderite (clay ironstone nodules); hosted by **coal-measure underclays and residual profiles** (see [Coal](../energy/coal.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** **refractories** — firebricks, crucibles, furnace/kiln linings, kiln furniture; high-duty ceramics.
- **Market:** refractory-grade clay commands a premium for **low iron and high alumina**.

## Exploration notes
- Target **underclays of coal measures** (see [Coal](../energy/coal.md)).
- Assay **refractoriness (PCE/orion), Al₂O₃**, and low Fe/alkali content.
- Grades into [Clay](clay.md) and [Kaolin](kaolin.md) depending on purity.

## Field checklist
- [ ] Resists high heat (refractory)?
- [ ] Plastic when wet, hard when fired?
- [ ] White/grey/buff (low iron)?
- [ ] Under a coal seam (seat earth)?
- [ ] Soft, earthy, fissile?

## Facts
- Fire clay is a **refractory** clay — it resists very high heat.
- It is used to make **firebricks** and kiln/furnace linings.
- It often occurs as **underclays ("seat earths") beneath coal seams**.
- Low **iron** and high **alumina** make the best refractory grade.
