# Kaolin (China Clay)

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic · **Mineral:** kaolinite Al₂Si₂O₅(OH)₄
> **Quick ID:** White to cream, soft, soapy and earthy; **plastic when wet**, hard and porous when fired; low iron (whiteness = quality).

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | White (high-grade) to cream |
| Feel | Soft, soapy, earthy, smooth |
| Hardness | ~2 (actual crystals only visible under a microscope) |
| Plasticity | **Plastic when wet**; hard & porous when fired |
| Key test | White + soapy + plastic when wet + low iron |

## What it is
A **white clay** dominated by the mineral **kaolinite**, formed by the intense weathering of feldspar-rich rocks (granites). Nigeria's most valuable clay resource.

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Kaolinite** — Al₂Si₂O₅(OH)₄, a 1:1 layer silicate (kaolin group) clay. It forms when feldspar (and other alumino-silicates) weathers, leaching away alkalis and silica and concentrating aluminium. Pure kaolin is white; iron staining makes it cream/yellow.

## Crystal habit
Kaolinite crystals are **microscopic platy** (hexagonal) sheets — invisible to the naked eye. In hand specimen it is **earthy, massive, and fine-grained**. You identify the *rock* (white, soapy clay), not individual crystals.

## Physical properties
- **Colour:** white (high-grade) to cream.
- **Feel:** soft, soapy, and earthy.
- **Plasticity:** plastic when wet; hard and porous when fired.
- Fine and smooth; low iron content (whiteness = quality).

## How to identify in the field
1. **Colour:** white to cream (iron-stained = lower grade).
2. **Feel:** soft, soapy, smooth, earthy.
3. **Plasticity:** makes a plastic paste with water.
4. **Firing:** turns hard and porous when fired (porcelain-like).
5. **Context:** deep weathering profiles over granite.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Brick/pottery clay** | Brick clay is coloured and coarser; kaolin is white, fine, and soapy. |
| **Bentonite** | Bentonite swells greatly in water; kaolin does not. |
| **Chalk / limestone** | Chalk and limestone **fizz** with acid; kaolin does not. |

## Genesis
Forms by intense **tropical weathering (lateritization)** of feldspar-rich rocks — feldspar alters to kaolinite as alkalis and silica are leached out. Deep, well-drained profiles over granite give the best, whitest kaolin.

## States / localities
Formed by deep tropical weathering across the Basement: **Ondo, Ekiti, Ogun, Edo, Delta, Oyo, Osun, Kwara, Kogi, Enugu**, and more. Nigeria is a major kaolin producer.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Feldspar (the source mineral), quartz, halloysite, laterite; hosted by **deep weathering profiles over granite** (see [Feldspar](feldspar.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** **ceramics** (porcelain, tiles), **paper coating**, **paint, rubber, plastics**, **refractories**, and **cosmetics**.
- **Market:** whiter, finer kaolin commands a premium; Nigeria's most valuable clay.

## Exploration notes
- Assess **whiteness, plasticity, and iron content** (lower iron = higher value).
- Often associated with **laterite profiles** (see [Laterite](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/sedimentary/laterite-ferricrete.md)).
- Distinguished from common brick clay by its **white colour and fine, soapy feel**.

## Field checklist
- [ ] White to cream?
- [ ] Soft, soapy, fine, earthy?
- [ ] Plastic when wet?
- [ ] No acid fizz?
- [ ] Deep weathering profile over granite?

## Facts
- Kaolin is Nigeria's **most valuable clay** resource.
- It forms by **weathering of feldspar** — "china clay" is altered granite.
- **Whiteness** and **low iron** = higher quality and value.
- It is essential for **porcelain, paper coating, and cosmetics**.
