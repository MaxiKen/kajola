# Trona (Natural Soda Ash)

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic (carbonate) · **Formula:** Na₃(CO₃)(HCO₃)·2H₂O
> **Quick ID:** Soft (2½–3), white/grey/yellow, **salty-alkaline taste**, soluble in water; becomes powdery (**efflorescent**) in dry air.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | White, grey, or yellow |
| Hardness | 2½–3 (soft) |
| Taste | Salty-alkaline |
| Solubility | Soluble in water; **efflorescent** (powders in dry air) |
| Lustre | Vitreous |
| Key test | Salty-alkaline + soluble + efflorescent |

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Na₃(CO₃)(HCO₃)·2H₂O** — sodium sesquicarbonate, a natural form of **soda ash**. Heating converts it to sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃) and sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃). Trona is the most common natural source of soda ash.

## Crystal habit
Tabular to prismatic crystals, fibrous, or massive **evaporite beds**. It commonly forms in alkaline lake deposits, often as crusts and beds.

## Physical properties
- **Hardness:** 2½–3 (soft).
- **Lustre:** vitreous; white, grey, or yellow.
- **Taste:** salty-alkaline; **soluble in water**; becomes powdery (**efflorescent**) in dry air.

## How to identify in the field
1. **Taste:** **salty-alkaline** (distinct from purely salty halite).
2. **Solubility:** dissolves in water.
3. **Efflorescence:** turns powdery in dry air (loses water).
4. **Hardness:** soft (2½–3).
5. **Context:** alkaline evaporite lakes/basins.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Halite (salt)** | Halite is purely salty and not alkaline; trona is salty-**alkaline** and efflorescent. |
| **Gypsum** | Gypsum is not soluble in the same way and not alkaline; trona is soluble and alkaline. |
| **Natron** | Natron (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) is more soluble and efflorescent; trona is less hydrated. |

## Genesis
**Evaporite deposits of alkaline saline lakes** — the same setting as the Lake Chad (Borno) evaporite belt (see [Salt](salt.md), [Brine](brine.md)). Trona precipitates in alkaline (soda) lakes as they evaporate.

## States / localities
**Borno, Yobe** — the Lake Chad/NE Nigeria evaporite province.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Halite, gypsum, natron, thenardite; hosted by **alkaline evaporite basins/lakes** (see [Salt](salt.md), [Gypsum](gypsum.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** **soda ash** (glass, detergents, chemicals), **baking soda**, water softening, pH control, metallurgy.
- **Market:** a valuable alternative soda-ash source; often co-mined with halite.

## Exploration notes
- Look in **alkaline evaporite basins/lakes**, associated with halite, gypsum, and natron (see [Salt](salt.md), [Gypsum](gypsum.md)).
- Assay **Na₂CO₃ / NaHCO₃** content; assess solubility and processing route.
- Target the Lake Chad/NE Nigeria evaporite province.

## Facts
- Trona is a natural form of **soda ash** (sodium carbonate).
- It has a distinctive **salty-alkaline** taste.
- It is **efflorescent** — turns powdery in dry air.
- It forms in **alkaline saline lakes** — the Lake Chad evaporite province.
