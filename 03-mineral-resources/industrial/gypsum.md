# Gypsum (Plaster & Cement)

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic · **Formula:** CaSO₄·2H₂O
> **Quick ID:** Very **soft (scratched by a fingernail, hardness 2)**, white/clear/grey, and **does NOT fizz** with acid (unlike limestone/calcite).

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | White, clear, grey |
| Hardness | **2** (scratched by a fingernail) |
| Acid test | **Does NOT fizz** (unlike calcite) |
| Cleavage | Perfect in one direction |
| Habit | Tabular (selenite), fibrous (satin spar), massive (alabaster) |
| Key test | Soft + no fizz + white/clear |

## What it is
A soft **evaporite mineral** (hydrated calcium sulfate), deposited by the evaporation of seas and lakes. Varieties include clear **selenite**, fibrous **satin spar**, and fine **alabaster**.

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Gypsum** — CaSO₄·2H₂O (hydrated calcium sulfate). An **evaporite** mineral — it precipitates as seawater or lake water evaporates. Heating gypsum drives off water to make **plaster of Paris**.

## Crystal habit
**Tabular** (selenite — clear, flat crystals), **fibrous** (satin spar — silky fibres), or **massive** (alabaster — fine-grained). Clear, diamond-shaped (selenite) crystals are common and collectable.

## Physical properties
- **Hardness 2** — very soft; scratched by a **fingernail**.
- **Does NOT fizz** with acid (unlike limestone/calcite).
- White/clear/grey; may form clear tabular crystals or fibrous veins.
- Perfect cleavage in one direction (flexible but not elastic sheets).

## How to identify in the field
1. **Hardness:** scratched by a fingernail (2) — very soft.
2. **Acid:** **no fizz** (calcite fizzes).
3. **Colour:** white, clear (selenite), or grey.
4. **Habit:** tabular, fibrous, or massive beds.
5. **Context:** evaporite beds with salt.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Calcite / limestone** | Calcite **fizzes** with acid and is harder (3); gypsum does not fizz and is soft (2). |
| **Halite (rock salt)** | Halite tastes salty and dissolves readily; gypsum does not taste salty. |
| **Anhydrite** | Anhydrite is harder (3.5) and lacks water; gypsum is softer (2). |

## Genesis
**Evaporite** — deposited by the evaporation of restricted seas and lakes, often interbedded with **salt (halite)** and **anhydrite**. Nigeria's gypsum formed in the **Chad Basin** and other evaporite settings.

## States / localities
- **Gombe** (Nafada, Dukku) — the main gypsum district.
- **Nasarawa** (Awe), **Bauchi**, **Borno** (Chad Basin), **Sokoto**.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Halite (salt), anhydrite, limestone, dolomite; hosted by **evaporite sequences** (see [Rock salt](salt.md), [Limestone](limestone.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **PLASTER** and **plasterboard** (the main use).
- **Cement** additive (retarder), **fertilizer**, and industrial filler.
- Gombe is a major Nigerian gypsum-producing district.

## Exploration notes
- Look for **soft, non-fizzing, white/clear** evaporite beds.
- Distinguished from **calcite/limestone** by **softness + no acid fizz**.
- Often interbedded with **salt** — worth checking for associated halite.

## Facts
- Gypsum is **very soft** — scratched by a fingernail (hardness 2).
- **No acid fizz** — the key test separating it from calcite/limestone.
- Heated gypsum becomes **plaster of Paris** (loses water).
- **Gombe (Nafada, Dukku)** is Nigeria's main gypsum district.
