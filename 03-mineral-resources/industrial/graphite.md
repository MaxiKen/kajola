# Graphite (Carbon)

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic (native element) · **Formula:** C (hexagonal)
> **Quick ID:** Very soft (1–2), **greasy** feel, grey-black; **stains paper and fingers grey**; conducts electricity (unusual for a non-metal).

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Grey-black (metallic to earthy) |
| Streak | Grey-black (stains) |
| Hardness | **1–2** (very soft) |
| Feel | **Greasy** to the touch |
| Conductivity | **Conducts electricity** |
| Key test | Soft + greasy + stains grey + conducts |

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Carbon (C)**, hexagonal — a native element mineral. The stable high-grade form of carbon (vs diamond). Fixed-carbon content defines grade. Graphite and diamond are both pure carbon but with different atomic structures.

## Crystal habit
**Foliated / scaly** (flake graphite), massive, or earthy (amorphous graphite). Flake graphite (in metamorphic rocks) is the highest-value form.

## Physical properties
- **Hardness:** very soft (1–2); **greasy** to the touch.
- **Lustre:** metallic to earthy; grey-black.
- **Signature:** **stains paper and fingers grey/black** (this is why it's "lead" in pencils).
- **Conducts electricity** (unusual for a non-metal).

## How to identify in the field
1. **Feel:** distinctly **greasy** to the touch.
2. **Stain:** rubs off grey-black on paper/fingers (like a pencil).
3. **Hardness:** very soft (1–2).
4. **Conductivity:** conducts electricity (unusual for a mineral).
5. **Context:** metamorphic schist/gneiss, carbonaceous rocks.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Molybdenite** | Molybdenite is bluish-grey (not pure grey) and also greasy; graphite is darker grey-black. |
| **Mica** | Mica peels into elastic sheets and does not stain; graphite stains grey. |
| **Talc** | Talc is soapy and white/grey but does not stain black. |

## Genesis
Metamorphism of **carbonaceous sediments** (graphite schist/gneiss), hydrothermal deposits, and rarely igneous sources. Often concentrated in schist belts — heat and pressure convert organic carbon to graphite.

## States / localities
**Kaduna, Niger, Plateau, Kogi** — reported in carbonaceous metasediments; a **battery-graphite** interest area (emerging).

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Mica, quartz, garnet, feldspar; hosted by **graphite schist/gneiss and carbonaceous metasediments** (see [Mica schist & Phyllite](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/metamorphic/mica-schist-phyllite.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** **Li-ion batteries** (fast-growing), pencils, lubricants, refractories, electrodes, brake linings.
- **Market:** flake graphite commands a premium; demand surging with EVs.

## Exploration notes
- Target **graphite schist/gneiss** and carbonaceous metasediments in the schist belts (see [Mica schist & Phyllite](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/metamorphic/mica-schist-phyllite.md)).
- Confirm by softness, greasy feel, and grey streak; **assay fixed carbon** for grade.
- Distinguish from **molybdenite** (bluish) and **mica** (elastic sheets).

## Field checklist
- [ ] Very soft (1–2) and greasy?
- [ ] Stains paper/fingers grey-black?
- [ ] Metallic to earthy lustre?
- [ ] Conducts electricity?
- [ ] In graphite schist / carbonaceous rock?

## Facts
- Graphite is the "**lead**" in pencils — it leaves a grey streak.
- It **conducts electricity** — unusual for a non-metal.
- **Flake graphite** (in metamorphic rock) is the highest-value form.
- Demand is surging with **Li-ion batteries** for EVs.
