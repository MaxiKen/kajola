# Quartz, Silica & Glass Sand

> **Group:** Industrial / non-metallic · **Formula:** SiO₂
> **Quick ID:** **Hardness 7** (scratches glass and steel), glassy, **no cleavage** (conchoidal fracture), **does not fizz**; white/clear/grey.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | White, clear, grey |
| Hardness | **7** (scratches glass and steel) |
| Cleavage | **None** (conchoidal fracture) |
| Lustre | Glassy |
| Acid test | Does **not** fizz |
| Key test | Hardness 7 + glassy + no cleavage |

## What it is
**Quartz** (silicon dioxide, SiO₂) — pure **silica**. As **glass sand** (high-purity quartz sand) it is the essential raw material for making glass.

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Quartz** — SiO₂ (silicon dioxide), a **tectosilicate**. The second most abundant mineral in the crust. Chemically very resistant, which is why quartz survives weathering and dominates sands. Varieties: amethyst, citrine, chalcedony, agate, flint.

## Crystal habit
**Hexagonal prisms** with pyramidal ends (crystals), or **massive/granular** (sand). In hand specimen, quartz often shows glassy, six-sided crystals; as sand it is rounded grains.

## Physical properties
- **Hardness 7** — scratches glass and steel.
- **Glassy** lustre; white/clear/grey; **no cleavage** (conchoidal fracture).
- **Does not fizz** with acid; chemically resistant.

## How to identify in the field
1. **Hardness:** 7 — scratches glass and steel.
2. **Cleavage:** **none** — breaks with curved (conchoidal) fracture.
3. **Lustre:** glassy.
4. **Acid:** no fizz.
5. **Context:** veins, granites, quartzite, or quartz sands.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Feldspar** | Feldspar has **two cleavages**; quartz has none (conchoidal fracture). |
| **Calcite** | Calcite **fizzes** with acid and is softer (3); quartz is harder (7) and does not fizz. |
| **Gypsum** | Gypsum is soft (2) and does not fizz; quartz is hard (7). |

## Genesis
Quartz forms in **igneous** (veins, granites, pegmatites), **metamorphic** (quartzite), and **sedimentary** (sandstone, sand) settings. It is the most durable common mineral, so it survives weathering and concentrates in sands.

## States / localities
Quartz from **granites, pegmatites, and quartzite** across the Basement; **glass-grade sand** from coastal/river sands — **Lagos, Ondo, Delta, Ogun**, and elsewhere.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Feldspar, mica (granite/pegmatite); hosted by **quartz veins, quartzite, and quartz sands** (see [Feldspar](feldspar.md), [Quartzite rock](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/metamorphic/quartzite-quartz-schist.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **GLASS** manufacture (the main use of glass sand).
- **Foundry** sand, **silicon metal**, **abrasives**, **filters**, and **fillers**.
- High-purity (low-iron) sand commands a premium.

## Exploration notes
- For **glass**, purity matters — assess **SiO₂ content and iron** (low iron = clear glass).
- Distinguish from **feldspar** (feldspar has two cleavages; quartz has none).
- Target quartz veins, quartzite, and pure quartz sands.

## Facts
- Quartz is **hardness 7** — it scratches glass.
- It has **no cleavage** — it breaks with curved (conchoidal) fracture.
- It is the raw material for **glass** (glass sand).
- Quartz is the **second most abundant mineral** in Earth's crust.
