# Lithium in Pegmatites (Spodumene & Lepidolite)

> **Group:** Industrial/metallic · **Minerals:** Spodumene LiAlSi₂O₆ · Lepidolite · **Element:** Lithium (Li)
> **Quick ID:** Spodumene = white/grey/green prismatic crystals (can be huge); lepidolite = soft lilac/pink mica that peels into sheets.

## At a glance

| Property | Spodumene | Lepidolite |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | White, grey, green (kunzite pink, hiddenite green) | **Lilac/pink** |
| Streak | White | White |
| Lustre | Vitreous | Pearly (mica) |
| Hardness | 6.5–7 | 2.5–4 (soft) |
| Cleavage | Perfect (prismatic) | Perfect (mica sheets) |
| Key test | Large prismatic crystals in pegmatite | Pink mica that peels |

## Chemistry & mineralogy
- **Spodumene** — a lithium **pyroxene**, LiAlSi₂O₆.
- **Lepidolite** — a lithium-rich **mica**.
Both are important **lithium ore minerals**, hosted in rare-element **pegmatites**.

## Crystal habit
**Spodumene:** prismatic, often **very large** (can be metres long); may be gemmy (**kunzite** = pink, **hiddenite** = green). **Lepidolite:** foliated, book-like mica.

## Physical properties
- **Spodumene:** hardness 6.5–7; white/grey/green; perfect cleavage; heavy.
- **Lepidolite:** hardness 2.5–4; soft, **lilac/pink** mica (peels into sheets).

## How to identify in the field
1. **Spodumene:** large, prismatic, white-grey-green crystals in pegmatite; hard (6.5–7).
2. **Lepidolite:** soft **lilac/pink mica** that peels into sheets — the giveaway.
3. **Setting:** zoned rare-element pegmatites.
4. **Association:** with columbite–tantalite, tourmaline, beryl.
5. **Crystal size:** spodumene crystals can be enormous (metres).

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Other pyroxenes (vs spodumene)** | Spodumene is typically very large and in lithium pegmatites; other pyroxenes are smaller and in different settings. |
| **Muscovite (vs lepidolite)** | Muscovite is colourless/silvery; lepidolite is distinctly **lilac/pink** and lithium-bearing. |
| **Beryl (vs spodumene)** | Beryl is hexagonal (6-sided) and often gemmy; spodumene is prismatic with different cleavage. |

## Genesis
**Magmatic–hydrothermal**, concentrated in the late, element-rich zones of **pegmatites** associated with the Younger Granites and older granitoids. Lithium concentrates in specific zones of the zoned pegmatite.

## States / localities
Pegmatite fields: **Plateau (Jos), Nasarawa, Kaduna, Bauchi, Oyo, Kogi**. Lithium is an **emerging** target in Nigerian pegmatites (sometimes called the new "white petroleum").

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Columbite–tantalite, cassiterite, tourmaline, beryl, feldspar, quartz; hosted by **zoned rare-element pegmatites** (see [Columbite–Tantalite](columbite-tantalite.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
The key ingredient in **rechargeable batteries** (EVs, phones, grid storage) — demand is booming. Also used in ceramics, glass, and greases; **kunzite/hiddenite** are gems.

## Exploration notes
- Map and sample **zoned pegmatites** — Li concentrates in specific zones.
- Look for **spodumene (white/green prisms)** and **lepidolite (pink mica)**.
- Often co-located with **columbite-tantalite** (see [Columbite–Tantalite](columbite-tantalite.md)).

## Field checklist
- [ ] Large prismatic spodumene crystals?
- [ ] Pink/lilac lepidolite mica?
- [ ] In a zoned rare-element pegmatite?
- [ ] With columbite-tantalite / tourmaline?
- [ ] Hardness test (spodumene 6.5–7 vs soft lepidolite)?

## Facts
- Lithium is the "**white petroleum**" — the key to EV and grid-storage batteries.
- **Spodumene** crystals can grow to **metres** in length in pegmatites.
- **Kunzite (pink)** and **hiddenite (green)** are gem varieties of spodumene.
- Nigerian lithium occurs in the **Younger Granite pegmatites** — an emerging target.
