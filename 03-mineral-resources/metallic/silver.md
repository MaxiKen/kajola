# Silver (Ag)

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Native silver** (Ag) and silver minerals — **argentite** Ag₂S, **tetrahedrite**.
In Nigeria, silver is recovered mainly **as a byproduct** of lead–zinc and gold ores.

## Crystal habit
**Native silver:** wire-like, dendritic, arborescent, or massive. **Argentite:** sooty
or massive, often replacing galena.

## Physical properties & how to test
- **Colour:** silver-white; **tarnishes black** on exposure.
- **Hardness:** soft (2½–3); very **heavy**; malleable and ductile.
- **Streak:** bright silvery-white (distinctive).
- Look for it as tiny specks or wire filaments in galena/gold ore.

## Genesis
Hydrothermal veins, especially the **enriched / oxidized zones** of lead–zinc and gold
systems (see [Galena & Sphalerite](../metallic/galena-sphalerite.md)).

## States / localities
**Plateau, Zamfara, Niger** — as a byproduct of Pb–Zn (e.g. Abakaliki, Zurak) and gold.
No dedicated silver mines; recovered during processing.

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** jewellery, electronics, solar panels, photography, medicine.
- **Market:** precious metal; valuable byproduct credit in polymetallic ores.

## Exploration notes
- **Assay Ag** alongside Pb, Zn, and Au in galena-rich and gossan samples.
- Silver enrichment often sits just above the main Pb–Zn zone.
- Distinguish from platinum/lead by softness and silvery streak (see [§5.2](../../05-field-identification/05-02-hand-specimen-tests.md)).
