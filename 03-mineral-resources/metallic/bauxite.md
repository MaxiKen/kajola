# Bauxite (Aluminium Ore)

> **Group:** Metallic (ore mixture) · **Minerals:** Gibbsite Al(OH)₃ · Boehmite & Diaspore AlO(OH) · **Element:** Aluminium (Al)
> **Quick ID:** Red-brown to white, **pisolitic** (pea-like) texture, soft, porous; forms in tropical laterite profiles.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Red-brown (iron-rich) to white/grey (high-alumina) |
| Texture | **Pisolitic / oolitic** (pea-like), porous, earthy |
| Hardness | Soft (1–3 gibbsite; up to 6–7 diaspore) |
| Acid test | No reaction |
| Magnetism | Not magnetic |
| Heft | Surprisingly light for a rock |
| Key test | Pisolitic texture + laterite setting |

## Chemistry & mineralogy
Not a single mineral but an **ore mixture**: aluminium hydroxides — **gibbsite** Al(OH)₃, **boehmite** & **diaspore** AlO(OH) — with iron oxides (hematite/goethite), kaolinite, and quartz. Rich bauxite carries ~35–60% Al₂O₃.

## Crystal habit
**Pisolitic / oolitic** (pea-sized concretions), massive, or earthy. Often shows a distinctive pisolitic (berry-like) texture — the hallmark of bauxite.

## Physical properties
- **Colour:** red-brown (iron-rich) to white/grey (high-alumina).
- **Hardness:** soft (1–3 for gibbsite; up to 6–7 for diaspore).
- **Texture:** porous, pisolitic, earthy — breaks into pea-like grains.
- **Heft:** surprisingly light for a rock; no reaction to acid; not magnetic.

## How to identify in the field
1. **Texture:** **pisolitic** — made of pea-sized (oolitic) concretions — the giveaway.
2. **Colour:** red-brown (iron-rich) to white (high-alumina).
3. **Heft:** light and porous.
4. **Acid/magnet:** no fizz, not magnetic.
5. **Setting:** tropical laterite profiles on plateaus.

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Laterite / ferricrete** | Laterite is more iron-rich (heavier, redder); bauxite is aluminium-rich (lighter, pisolitic). |
| **Clay / kaolin** | Clay is fine and plastic; bauxite is pisolitic (pea-like grains). |
| **Oolitic ironstone** | Ironstone is heavy and magnetic/red-streaked; bauxite is light and non-magnetic. |

## Genesis
Forms by intense **tropical lateritic weathering** of alumina-rich rocks — silica is leached away, leaving aluminium (and iron) concentrated near the surface (see [Regolith & laterite](../../01-general-geology/01-07-regolith-and-laterite.md)). Warm, wet climates and good drainage are essential.

## States / localities
**Ekiti, Ondo, Adamawa, Plateau, Oyo** — typically on laterite plateaus. Largely **underexploited** (Nigeria imports much of its alumina).

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Gibbsite, boehmite, diaspore, hematite/goethite, kaolinite, quartz; hosted by **laterite profiles** on alumina-rich bedrock (see [Laterite rock](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/sedimentary/laterite-ferricrete.md), [Kaolin](../industrial/kaolin.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** aluminium metal (Bayer + Hall–Héroult processes), refractories, cement, abrasives.
- **Market:** aluminium is strategic & high-value; **low-silica** bauxite commands a premium.

## Exploration notes
- Look for thick, **low-silica, high-Al₂O₃ laterite/bauxite** on plateaus.
- **Drill + assay** Al₂O₃, reactive SiO₂, Fe₂O₃; thickness and overburden matter.
- Associated with [Laterite](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/sedimentary/laterite-ferricrete.md) and [Kaolin](../../03-mineral-resources/industrial/kaolin.md).

## Facts
- Bauxite is the world's main **aluminium ore** — all aluminium metal starts as bauxite.
- Its **pisolitic** (pea-like) texture is the giveaway.
- It forms by intense **tropical weathering** that leaches silica and concentrates aluminium.
- Nigeria has bauxite but is largely **underexploited** (importing much of its alumina).
