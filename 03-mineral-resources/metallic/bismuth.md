# Bismuth (Bi)

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Native bismuth** (Bi) — a brittle, crystalline metalloid — and the sulfide
**bismuthinite** (Bi₂S₃). Often carries traces of As, S, Te.

## Crystal habit
**Hoppered (skeletal) cubic crystals**, dendritic, or massive. *Note:* the vivid
rainbow "hopper" bismuth is usually **lab-grown**; natural bismuth is rarer and duller.

## Physical properties & how to test
- **Colour:** silvery-white with pink/iridescent tarnish.
- **Hardness:** 2–2½ (soft but brittle).
- **Density:** very high (heavy for its size).
- **Signature:** **diamagnetic** (repelled by a magnet) and melts at a low ~271 °C.

## Genesis
Hydrothermal veins and pegmatites, commonly associated with **cobalt, nickel, silver,
lead, tin and tungsten** ores (e.g. the Jos Plateau pegmatites).

## States / localities
**Plateau, Nasarawa** — reported in pegmatite/hydrothermal systems; a **minor**,
byproduct-type occurrence.

## Mining, uses & market
- **Uses:** low-melting **fusible alloys**, **pharmaceuticals** (bismuth subsalicylate),
  cosmetics, **fire-sprinkler** alloys, non-toxic lead substitute.
- **Market:** specialty metal; recovered as a byproduct of Pb–W–Sn processing.

## Exploration notes
- Look for it as a **trace byproduct** in polymetallic and pegmatite systems
  (see [Cassiterite](../metallic/cassiterite.md), [Wolframite & Scheelite](../metallic/wolframite-scheelite.md)).
- **Assay Bi** in multi-element geochemistry of granitic/pegmatite ground.
