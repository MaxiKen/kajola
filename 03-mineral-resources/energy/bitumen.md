# Bitumen (Tar Sands)

> **Group:** Energy · **Material:** natural asphalt (heavy hydrocarbon)
> **Quick ID:** Thick, sticky, **black heavy hydrocarbon** that coats sand grains; smells of **petroleum**; very viscous.

## At a glance

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Black, sticky, tar-like; coats sand grains |
| Smell | **Petroleum** odour |
| Viscosity | Very high (does not flow like oil) |
| Host | "Tar sands" / "oil sands" (sand coated with bitumen) |
| Key test | Black sticky petroleum-smelling sand |

## What it is
**Bitumen** — a thick, sticky, black **heavy hydrocarbon** that coats sand grains in "tar sands" or "oil sands." A very viscous form of petroleum and a potential **unconventional oil resource**.

## Chemistry & mineralogy
**Bitumen** — the heaviest fraction of petroleum: a complex mix of large hydrocarbon molecules (high carbon, high molecular weight). It is too viscous to pump at surface temperatures — unlike crude oil.

## Crystal habit
**Amorphous** — bitumen is not crystalline. It occurs as a **coating on sand grains** (tar sands) or filling fractures/veins (solid asphalt). The sand-plus-bitumen mixture is the "tar sand."

## Physical properties
- **Colour:** black, sticky, tar-like.
- **Smell:** distinctly of **petroleum**.
- **Viscosity:** very high — thick and sticky (does not flow like oil).
- **Behaviour:** coats and cements sand grains.

## How to identify in the field
1. **Appearance:** black, sticky, tar-like material.
2. **Smell:** petroleum odour.
3. **Texture:** coats/cements sand grains (tar sand).
4. **Viscosity:** thick and sticky (not free-flowing).
5. **Context:** oil-bearing basins (updip of oil accumulations).

## Lookalikes & how to distinguish

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Coal** | Coal is solid, brittle, and not sticky/oily; bitumen is sticky and petroleum-smelling. |
| **Crude oil** | Crude oil is a free-flowing liquid; bitumen is thick and sticky (semi-solid). |
| **Black soil/ shale** | Shale/soil lacks the sticky, petroleum-smelling, tar-like character. |

## Genesis
Forms by the **biodegradation and weathering of crude oil** — the lighter fractions are lost (washed out or consumed by microbes), leaving the heavy, viscous bitumen. Bitumen typically accumulates in the **updip, shallow** parts of oil basins.

## States / localities
- **Ondo State** — very large bitumen/tar-sand deposits (Akoko/Ikale area), among the biggest in the region.
- **Edo, Lagos, Ogun** — the southwestern (Dahomey Basin) bitumen belt.

## Associated minerals & host rocks
Quartz sand (the host), clay, water; hosted by **tar-sand (oil-sand) deposits** in the Dahomey Basin (see [Petroleum](petroleum.md), [The sedimentary basins](../../01-general-geology/01-04-sedimentary-basins.md)).

## Mining, uses & market
- **Road surfacing / asphalt** — the immediate use.
- A significant **potential unconventional oil resource** — Ondo's deposits could yield large volumes of heavy oil if developed.

## Exploration notes
- **Black, sticky, petroleum-smelling** sand (see [Bitumen (rock)](../../02-rocks-of-nigeria/sedimentary/bitumen.md)).
- Development depends on **recovery technology and oil price** (heavy oil is costlier to extract).
- **Ondo's** bitumen belt is among the largest in the region.

## Facts
- Bitumen is the **heaviest, most viscous** form of petroleum.
- **Ondo State** has very large bitumen/tar-sand deposits.
- It is used immediately for **road asphalt**.
- It is a potential **unconventional oil** resource (heavy oil).
