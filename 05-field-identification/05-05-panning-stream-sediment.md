# 5.5 — Panning & Stream-Sediment Sampling

> **What it is:** The cheapest, fastest way to find **heavy minerals** — gold, cassiterite, and more — by letting the river concentrate them, then **panning**.
> **Golden rule:** Sample **systematically** and **follow the anomaly upstream** toward the source.

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Principle | Dense minerals stay in the pan; light sand/silt washes away |
| Best for | Gold, cassiterite, magnetite, ilmenite, zircon, monazite |
| Method | Systematic panning / stream-sediment survey |

| Key move | **Follow the anomaly upstream** to the lode source |

The cheapest, fastest way to find **heavy minerals** — gold, cassiterite, and more — is to let the river do the concentrating for you, then **pan**.

## Why it works
Heavy minerals (gold, cassiterite, magnetite, ilmenite, zircon, monazite) stay in the pan while light sand/silt washes away. Rivers are natural gravity concentrators — they sort minerals by density along their course.

## How to pan (gold / heavy mineral)
1. Fill the pan with stream gravel/sand; submerge in water.
2. Break up lumps; shake gently to settle heavy minerals to the bottom.
3. Tilt and swirl; wash off the light top layer repeatedly.
4. Carefully pick out the **concentrate** — black sands (magnetite/ilmenite), and look for **gold flakes** (yellow, soft) or **cassiterite** (brown-black, hard).

## Strategy
- **Sample systematically** — at regular intervals along a stream or on a grid, so you can spot a **geochemical anomaly** (stream-sediment survey).
- **Follow the anomaly upstream** — the concentration builds toward the source (the lode).
- The **−80 mesh heavy-mineral fraction** is the standard sample for assay.

## What the concentrate tells you
| Concentrate | Indicates |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Gold flakes/grains** | Gold nearby (upstream / in drainage) |
| **Cassiterite** | Tin (pegmatite/placer) upstream |
| **Magnetite / black sand** | Fe-Ti heavy minerals (coastal/beach sands) |
| **Zircon / monazite** | Rare-earth / Ti placer potential |

## Reading the pan (step by step)
1. **Note the black sands** — magnetite/ilmenite mark heavy-mineral concentration.
2. **Look for yellow, soft flakes** — gold (confirm: malleable, heavy, no tarnish).
3. **Look for brown-black hard grains** — cassiterite (confirm: very heavy, resinous).
4. **Trace it upstream** — heavy minerals accumulate toward their source; the "source" is your target.

## Field cautions
- Distinguish **gold vs pyrite/mica** (see [§5.2](05-02-hand-specimen-tests.md)).
- Contamination: avoid sampling near old workings or machinery.
- Sample a **consistent** location type (active channel vs bank) to avoid bias.

## Common pitfalls
- **Mistaking pyrite for gold** — pyrite is brittle and hard; gold is malleable.
- **Sampling near old workings/machinery** — causes false (contaminant) anomalies.
- **Not going far enough upstream** — trace the anomaly to its source.

## Facts
- Rivers are **natural gravity concentrators** — panning lets them work for you.
- The **−80 mesh heavy-mineral fraction** is the standard assay sample.
- **Follow the anomaly upstream** — concentration builds toward the lode.
- **Black sands** (magnetite/ilmenite) flag heavy-mineral ground.
