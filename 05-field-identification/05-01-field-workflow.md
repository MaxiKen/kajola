# 5.1 — The Field Workflow

> **What it is:** The step-by-step exploration funnel — from a huge region down to a drill hole.
> **Golden rule:** Work the steps **in order**; never skip straight to sampling. *Describe before you interpret.*

## At a glance

| Stage | Purpose | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prepare | Know the ground before you go | Maps, play type, permits, logistics |
| 2. Reconnaissance | See the geology & clues firsthand | Lithology/structure maps, float, gossans |
| 3. Target generation | Turn data into ranked anomalies | Geochem/geophys anomalies, pan results |
| 4. Follow-up & drilling | Test the best targets at depth | Detailed maps, assays, drill logs |
| 5. Record & report | Preserve defensible data | Notebook, photos, QA/QC, report |

Exploration is a **funnel**: from a huge area down to a drill hole. Work the steps in order; never skip straight to sampling.

## 1. Prepare
- Review the **geological map** ([§1.8](../01-general-geology/01-08-geological-map-of-nigeria.md)), GSN bulletins, NGGS data, and previous reports.
- Identify the **play type** you're chasing (see [Part IV provinces](../04-geological-provinces/README.md)) — e.g. schist-belt gold, pegmatite tin/coltan, Benue Pb–Zn.
- Plan access, permits, and logistics; check the season (Harmattan dust; wet-season mud).

## 2. Regional reconnaissance
- **Traverse** on foot/vehicle; **map lithology and structures** (foliation, folds, faults, veins) onto a base map/GPS.
- Note **alteration, gossans, old workings, and artisanal pits** — often the best clues.
- Collect **rock float** and make a first-pass **hand-specimen ID** (see [§5.2](05-02-hand-specimen-tests.md)).

## 3. Target generation
- Integrate mapping + geochemistry + geophysics into **anomalies** (see [§5.4](05-04-regolith-geochem-dispersion.md)).
- **Pan streams** to detect heavy minerals (see [§5.5](05-05-panning-stream-sediment.md)).
- Rank targets by size, grade, and confidence.

## 4. Follow-up & drilling
- Detailed mapping + infill sampling on the best targets.
- **Auger/RC/diamond drilling** to test at depth; log core carefully.
- Assay samples at an accredited lab (Au by fire-assay, multi-element by ICP).

## 5. Record & report
- **Field notebook**: date, location (GPS), lithology, structure, mineralization, alteration, regolith, sample numbers.
- **Photos**: always include a scale (hammer, coin, hand lens) and GPS.
- **QA/QC**: blanks, standards, field duplicates; chain of custody for samples.

## Equipment checklist
- **ID tools:** 10× hand lens, steel knife, streak plate, hand magnet, dilute HCl.
- **Mapping:** GPS, base maps, compass/clinometer, field notebook, camera.
- **Sampling:** rock hammer, chisel, sample bags, sample tags, marker pens.
- **Safety:** see [§5.7 Safety](05-07-safety-asm.md).

## Golden rules
- *Describe before you interpret.*
- *Every sample gets a unique number and a location.*
- *The anomaly doesn't lie — but the contaminator does (check for nail/galvanized contamination).*

## Common pitfalls
- **Skipping to sampling** before understanding the geology.
- **Mixing sample horizons** — makes geochemistry noisy.
- **Contamination** — galvanized tools, nails, or dirty bags ruin assays.
- **No scale in photos** — a photo without scale is nearly useless.

## Facts
- Exploration is a **funnel** — broad regional work narrows to a drill hole.
- **Old workings and artisanal pits** are often the best clues.
- Every sample needs a **unique number + GPS location**.
- **QA/QC** (blanks, standards, duplicates) makes your data defensible.
