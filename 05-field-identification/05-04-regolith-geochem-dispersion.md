# 5.4 — Regolith & Geochemical Dispersion

> **What it is:** In Nigeria's deep **tropical weathering**, mineralization is hidden beneath a thick regolith. Understanding it is *the* key to exploration here.
> **Golden rule:** Sample a **consistent regolith horizon** — mixing horizons makes noise.

In Nigeria's deep **tropical weathering**, mineralization is hidden beneath a thick regolith. Understanding it is *the* key to exploration here.

## The regolith profile (top → bottom)
- **Laterite / ferricrete** — hard, rusty iron-rich cap.
- **Mottled zone** — red/white/pale mottling.
- **Pallid (bleached) zone** — pale, clay-rich, leached.
- **Saprolite** — rotted rock retaining original texture.
- **Fresh rock** — unweathered bedrock.

## Geochemical dispersion
- **Primary** — in the ore itself.
- **Secondary** — in the weathered rock/soil above the ore (clastic & hydromorphic).
- **Hydromorphic** — elements carried in solution and re-precipitated (a "halo").

In lateritic terrain, some elements are **residually enriched** (e.g. iron, aluminium, tin), while others are **leached away** — so geochemistry must be interpreted with the profile in mind.

## Sampling methods
- **Soil sampling** — regular grid or traverse; −80 mesh fraction; the mainstay of regional geochemistry.
- **Profile pits / auger** — to sample the right regolith horizon (often the saprolite/laterite interface).
- **Laterite/nodule sampling** — can carry metal anomalies.
- **Termite mounds** — a useful, cheap "deep" sample (termites bring up subsoil).

## Why termite mounds work (a Nigerian trick)
Termites excavate deep subsoil and bring it to the surface, so a **termite mound samples depth you can't easily reach** with a hand auger — a cheap, effective "deep" geochemical sample widely used in tropical exploration.

## Tips
- Sample a **consistent horizon** across a survey — mixing horizons makes noise.
- In **gold** ground, coarse gold causes "nugget effect" — take larger samples and use field duplicates.
- Look for **lateritized/canga caps** above iron and manganese.

## Common pitfalls
- **Mixing regolith horizons** across a survey — creates false anomalies/noise.
- **Ignoring the nugget effect** in gold ground — use larger samples + duplicates.
- **Forgetting leaching** — some elements (e.g. copper) are stripped from the profile.

## Facts
- Nigeria's deep **tropical regolith** hides mineralization beneath a thick profile.
- Sample a **consistent horizon** — mixing horizons ruins geochemistry.
- **Termite mounds** give a cheap "deep" geochemical sample.
- Some elements are **residually enriched** (Fe, Al, Sn); others are **leached** (Cu).
