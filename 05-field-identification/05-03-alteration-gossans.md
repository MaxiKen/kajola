# 5.3 — Alteration & Gossans

> **What it is:** Alteration is the **halo** around an ore body — often the biggest, easiest target you'll ever see. Learn to read it.
> **Golden rule:** Map the alteration **zoning** and walk *toward* the ore.

## Gossans (iron-oxide caps)
When a sulfide ore body weathers, it leaves a rusty **iron-oxide (limonite/goethite) cap** at the surface.
- **Colour:** red-brown, ochre yellow, orange, or chocolate brown.
- **Texture:** porous, spongy, cellular, or honeycombed — with **boxworks** (the dissolved-away casts of sulfide grains).
- **Where:** especially above **lead–zinc, iron, and some gold** deposits (see [Galena & Sphalerite](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/galena-sphalerite.md), [Iron ore](../03-mineral-resources/metallic/iron-ore.md)).

> ⚠️ **Gossan vs laterite:** both are rusty and red, but a **gossan** sits over sulfide rock and has **boxworks/porosity**; **laterite** is a general tropical weathering crust (see [§5.4](05-04-regolith-geochem-dispersion.md)).

## Hydrothermal alteration types
| Alteration | Looks like | Associated with |
|------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Silicification** | Hard, cherty, quartz-rich | Gold, tin, base metals |
| **Potassic** (K-feldspar/biotite) | Pinkish, fresh feldspar | Porphyry core zones |
| **Phyllic / sericitic** | Silvery sericite mica sheen | Gold, porphyry |
| **Argillic / kaolitic** | Soft, white clayey | Near-surface, epithermal gold |
| **Chloritic** | Green, soft | Base metals |
| **Propylitic** | Pale green, low-grade | Outer halo |
| **Carbonate** | Calcite/veins, fizzes | Base metals, gold |

## Alteration zoning
Around many deposits, alteration is **zoned** — e.g. a **potassic core → phyllic → argillic → propylitic** outward. Mapping the zoning vector helps you walk *toward* the ore. In the **schist-belt gold** ground, look for **silicified, sericite-altered, pyritic zones** near shear zones.

## How to read a gossan (step by step)
1. **Confirm it's a gossan, not laterite** — look for **boxworks** (cellular casts of dissolved sulfides) and porosity.
2. **Chip fresh rock** — a gossan can be a thin crust over fresh sulfide.
3. **Note iron staining along fractures/veins** — it follows fluid pathways.
4. **Sample altered rock** and assay **pathfinders** (As, Sb, Cu, Zn, Pb, Bi).

## Field tips
- Chip fresh rock (gossans can be just a thin crust).
- Note **iron staining along fractures/veins** and **clay** that might mask mineralization.
- **Pathfinder elements** (As, Sb, Cu, Zn, Pb, Bi) can be assayed in altered rock.

## Common pitfalls
- **Mistaking laterite for a gossan** — laterite lacks boxworks and sulfide-hosted porosity.
- **Only sampling the crust** — always chip to fresh rock beneath.
- **Ignoring clay** — argillic alteration can mask mineralization but is itself a clue.

## Facts
- A **gossan** is the rusty iron-oxide cap over a weathered sulfide ore body.
- **Boxworks** (cellular casts of dissolved sulfides) are the giveaway texture.
- Alteration is **zoned** — mapping it points you *toward* the ore.
- **Pathfinder elements** (As, Sb, Cu, Zn, Pb, Bi) confirm the alteration halo.
